# Finding Leaks Early

> Second note of the plumbing arc — the proactive backbone: the whole-house water-meter test plus a five-spot inspection routine that catches slow leaks before they rot a [[GLOSSARY#Subfloor|subfloor]] or stain the ceiling.

## Summary
- Two skills: (1) the **whole-house water-meter test** — answers "is there a leak anywhere?" in 30 seconds, and (2) the **five-spot inspection** — where and what to check.
- **The meter test:** shut off every water user, watch the [[GLOSSARY#Water meter leak indicator|leak indicator]] (the small triangle/star wheel on the meter face) for 30s–2min. Motionless = no leak; moving = hunt with the inspection. Higher-sensitivity version: note meter digits, wait 30–60 min, recheck.
- Meter moves with everything off but you find nothing inside → suspect a leak **between meter and house** (underground supply line). Look for a perpetually wet/green yard patch. Pro-only — don't dig.
- **Five spots:** (1) under every sink (feel cabinet floors, check the [[GLOSSARY#P-trap|P-trap]] + [[GLOSSARY#Supply line (supply tube)|supply lines]]), (2) around every toilet (failed [[GLOSSARY#Wax ring (toilet)|wax ring]] = water at base *only when flushed*; hissing = [[GLOSSARY#Flapper (toilet)|flapper]] leak), (3) the [[GLOSSARY#T&P valve (temperature & pressure relief valve)|water heater]] (T&P drip, rust at base), (4) washing machine + dishwasher hoses, (5) fixtures/tubs/ceilings below bathrooms.
- **Toilet dye test on every toilet:** 10 drops food coloring in the tank, wait 15–30 min, don't flush. Color in bowl = leaking [[GLOSSARY#Flapper (toilet)|flapper]] — silent 24/7 leak, top cause of mystery high bills.
- Cadence: **monthly (10 min)** meter test + visual sweep; **quarterly (20 min)** full checklists. Always act on a surprise — musty smell, soft floor, high bill.
- Hidden-leak red flags: unexplained high bill, sound of running water, musty smell, warm/damp floor, mold, water stains or bubbling paint, sudden cracks.
- Feeds [[Supply Lines, Hoses & Shut-off Valves|supply-line replacement]] (the fix for many of the leaks you'll find) and the [[Water Heater Maintenance|water-heater]] inspection slot.

## The two skills
1. **Whole-house water meter test** — answers "is there a leak anywhere?" in 30 seconds.
2. **The five-spot inspection routine** — where and what to check, monthly/quarterly.

## Skill 1 — The water meter test
The single best diagnostic in residential plumbing.

### Find the meter
Ground-level box in front yard near the street, or basement/utility room on older homes. Under the lid: the dial + the [[GLOSSARY#Water meter leak indicator|leak indicator]] (small triangle/star wheel).

### The test
1. Turn off **every** water user (faucets, dishwasher, washer, ice maker, sprinklers, toilets not refilling).
2. Watch the leak indicator for 30s–2min.
3. **Motionless = no leak.** **Moving = leak somewhere — hunt with skill 2.**

Higher-sensitivity version: note the meter digits, wait 30–60 min with no water use, recheck. Changed reading = leak (catches even tiny ones).

> ⚠️ If meter moves with everything off but you find nothing inside → suspect a leak **between meter and house** (underground supply line). Look for a perpetually wet/unusually green yard patch. Pro-only — don't dig.

## Skill 2 — The five-spot inspection

### 1. Under every sink
- Pull everything out of the cabinet
- Feel the cabinet floor (back corners) for damp
- Look for stains, warped wood, mold
- Run faucet; check trap + supply lines for drips
- Look for green corrosion / mineral crust on metal

### 2. Around every toilet
- Floor around base for water/staining (failed [[GLOSSARY#Wax ring (toilet)|wax ring]] leaks at base *only when flushed*)
- Supply line connection behind toilet for moisture
- Listen for hissing/running (flapper leak → dye test)
- Open tank: water spilling into overflow tube?
- Caulk/seal at floor for gaps

### 3. The water heater
- Floor / drain pan under the tank
- Inlet/outlet connections on top for moisture
- [[GLOSSARY#T&P valve (temperature & pressure relief valve)|T&P valve]] pipe end for drip
- Tank base for rust trails (drips from base often = tank failing — before-it-floods warning)

### 4. Washing machine & dishwasher
- Behind/under washer for pooling
- Both hot/cold supply hoses — bulges, cracks, damp
- Dishwasher supply + drain under the kitchen sink
- Run each; watch for drips during cycle

### 5. Fixtures, tubs, ceilings
- Caulk gaps around tubs, showers, sink rims
- Ceilings/walls **below** bathrooms for stains or soft spots
- Run shower; check wall behind the handle
- Sniff for musty smells near plumbing walls

## The cadence
- **Monthly (10 min):** meter test + quick visual sweep of all five spots
- **Quarterly (20 min):** full checklists above, including running fixtures + feeling cabinet floors
- **Always:** act on surprises — musty smell, soft floor spot, high water bill. Never nothing.

## The toilet dye test (run on every toilet)
Catches a leaking [[GLOSSARY#Flapper (toilet)|flapper]] — silent 24/7 leak, top cause of mystery high bills.
1. Remove tank lid (set somewhere safe — porcelain, model-specific)
2. Add 10 drops food coloring (or dye tablet) to tank water
3. Wait 15–30 min, **don't flush**
4. Color in bowl = flapper leaking. Fix is a $5 flapper.

## Signs of a hidden leak
- Unexplained high water bill (compare month-to-month, year-over-year)
- Sound of running water when nothing's on
- Musty / earthy smell in a specific room
- Warm or damp floor spot (often a hot-water line)
- Mold/mildew where it shouldn't be damp
- Water stains, bubbling/peeling paint on ceilings/walls
- Sudden cracks in walls/foundation
- Reduced pressure at one fixture (mineral buildup at leak point)

## DIY / Pro boundary
| DIY (detection) | Call a pro |
|-----|------------|
| Water meter test | Meter moves but nothing found inside |
| Toilet dye test | Suspected underground supply-line leak |
| Visual + tactile inspection of all 5 spots | Leak behind finished wall or under slab |
| Replacing flapper / supply line | Water heater tank leaking from base |
| Tightening a weeping compression fitting | Anything involving the main supply line |
