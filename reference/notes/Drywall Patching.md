# Drywall Patching

> First lesson of the drywall arc — the original [[MISSION]] commitment. Three keys: match method to hole size, choose the right compound, [[GLOSSARY#Feathering|feather]] the edges.

## Summary
Method follows hole size: spackle for nail/screw holes up to ½", [[GLOSSARY#Mesh tape (self-adhesive fiberglass)|mesh tape]] + compound for ½"–2", a [[GLOSSARY#California patch (hat patch)|California patch]] for 2"–5" doorknob holes, and cut-to-stud replacement for anything bigger. The single most important concept is the compound decision: [[GLOSSARY#Setting-type compound ("hot mud")|setting-type ("hot mud")]] cures by chemical reaction (no shrink, deep fills in one coat, hard to sand), while [[GLOSSARY#Drying-type compound (ready-mix / all-purpose)|drying-type (ready-mix)]] cures by evaporation (shrinks, easy to sand, the finish-coat standard). The pairing rule is non-negotiable — mesh tape **must** use setting-type (drying-type shrinkage cracks flexible mesh); [[GLOSSARY#Paper tape (drywall tape)|paper tape]] works under either but needs bedding-in. The visible skill is [[GLOSSARY#Feathering|feathering]] — each coat wider and thinner at the edges (4" → 8" → 12") so the patch tapers to nothing under your hand. The hardest part for beginners is [[GLOSSARY#Texture matching|texture matching]]; pre-1980 popcorn may contain asbestos — test before disturbing.

## The method by hole size
| Size | Method |
|---|---|
| Up to ½" (nail/screw/anchor) | Spackle + putty knife |
| ½"–2" (small impact, crack) | Mesh tape + setting compound |
| 2"–5" (doorknob) | [[GLOSSARY#California patch (hat patch)|California patch]] or patch kit |
| >5" (large impact) | Cut-to-stud replacement piece (separate lesson) |

## The material decision (most important concept)

| | [[GLOSSARY#Setting-type compound ("hot mud")|Setting-type ("hot mud")]] | [[GLOSSARY#Drying-type compound (ready-mix / all-purpose)|Drying-type (ready-mix)]] |
|---|---|---|
| Cures by | Chemical reaction | Water evaporation |
| Form | Powder, mix with water | Pre-mixed bucket |
| Time | 5–90 min (labeled) | 24 hr per coat |
| Shrinks? | No — fills deep gaps in one coat | Yes — deep fills need multiple coats |
| Sandable | Harder | Easier |
| Best for | First/taping coat, deep fills, mesh tape | Finish coats, skim, feathering |

> ⚠️ **The pairing rule:** mesh tape **must** use setting-type compound. Mesh is flexible; drying-type shrinkage over it cracks. Paper tape works under either.

### Setting times
- **20-min:** small patches, quick successive coats. Work fast.
- **45-min:** doorknob-size patches, beginner-friendly working time.
- **90-min:** larger repairs, max working time for beginners.
- Mix small batches — leftover hardens in the pan.

## Tiny holes (up to ½")
1. Press spackle (or joint compound) in with 1–2" putty knife. Overfill slightly.
2. Scrape flat with knife edge.
3. Dry (spackle ~1 hr, compound 24 hr).
4. Sand flush (220 grit or sanding sponge). Wipe dust.
5. Touch up paint.

> Wall-anchor hole through-and-through? Stuff paper towel in first so spackle has backing.

## Small holes + cracks (½"–2"): mesh + compound
1. Trim loose paper edges flat.
2. Cut [[GLOSSARY#Mesh tape (self-adhesive fiberglass)|mesh tape]] ~1" larger than hole all sides. Press adhesive flat over hole.
3. **Fill coat:** setting-type compound over mesh with 4–6" knife. Press in, scrape most back off, mesh filled flush. Flat, not pretty.
4. Let set (no longer cool to touch — reaction gives off heat).
5. **Second coat:** wider than first, [[GLOSSARY#Feathering|feathered]] thin at edges. 8" knife, progressively lower angle toward patch edge — compound tapers to nothing.
6. Optional third coat (drying-type) even wider.
7. **Sand** with fine sanding sponge, outward circles. Stop when patch feels flush under hand (close eyes, feel — hand detects lips eye misses).
8. **Prime, then paint.** Bare compound sucks paint differently → primer prevents "flash."

> **Feathering is the skill.** Each coat wider + thinner at edges than last. 4" fill → 8" second → 12" finish. Under-feathered = visible pimple; well-feathered = invisible.

## Doorknob holes (2"–5"): California patch
The pro trick. Patch's own paper becomes the tape — no separate taping step.
1. Cut drywall scrap square ~2" larger than hole all sides.
2. On the **back**, score a square the size of the hole + ½" margin, centered.
3. Snap off gypsum around the score, leaving **front paper intact** → gypsum plug + paper "flange" (a hat).
4. Trim gypsum plug to fit actual hole. Test-fit.
5. Apply setting compound to paper flange. Press plug into hole, flange flat to wall.
6. Squeeze out excess with knife (center outward), bedding flange into mud.
7. Let set. Apply wider finish coat over flange, feathered thin.
8. Sand, prime, paint.

> Alternative: self-adhesive patch kit (metal/fiberglass square, mud over). Easier, slightly more visible under certain light. California patch = invisible; kit = good-enough.

## Texture matching (the hardest part)
| Texture | How |
|---|---|
| Smooth | Sand flush, prime, paint. No texture step. |
| Orange peel | Aerosol spray (~$8). Practice on cardboard; adjust nozzle for droplet size. Dry, light sand, paint. |
| Knockdown | Spray orange peel, wait ~10 min, drag wide knife to flatten peaks. Harder — practice first. |
| Popcorn | Aerosol popcorn + Styrofoam chips. **Pre-1980 may contain asbestos — test before disturbing.** |

> ⚠️ **Pre-1980 popcorn ceiling = test for asbestos before sanding/scraping.** Safe if left alone; hazardous when disturbed. $15 test kit or lab sample. If asbestos, leave undisturbed or use licensed abatement pro.
