# Garage Door Maintenance

> The largest moving object in the home — a 150+ lb panel driven by a motor and counterbalanced by dangerous springs. Covers the homeowner-safe track (lube, clean, test) and the strictly-pro track (anything spring-related).

## Summary
The hard line: the [[GLOSSARY#Torsion spring|torsion spring]] (above the door) and [[GLOSSARY#Extension spring|extension spring]] (along the tracks) store enough energy to injure or kill — **any** spring work, cable replacement, and track realignment are 100% pro. The [[GLOSSARY#Opener (garage door opener)|opener]] just moves the door; the springs do the actual lifting, which is why a broken spring makes the door impossibly heavy. You can *diagnose* springs with the [[GLOSSARY#Balance test|balance test]] (pull the [[GLOSSARY#Emergency release cord|red release cord]], lift halfway, let go — a balanced door holds), but adjustment is pro-only.

The highest-leverage homeowner task is lubrication every 3–6 months: hinges, metal roller stems (not the wheels), a light coat on the springs, and the chain/screw drive — white lithium or silicone spray, never WD-40 (it's a solvent). **Do not lubricate the tracks** — lube there mixes with grit into a grinding paste that wears rollers faster; wipe tracks clean and dry only.

The federally-mandated safety systems (required on all openers since 1993) get a monthly test: the [[GLOSSARY#Auto-reverse (mechanical)|mechanical auto-reverse]] with a 2x4 flat on the floor (door must reverse on contact), and the [[GLOSSARY#Photo eye (photoelectric sensor)|photo-eye]] beam by waving an object through it while closing (must be ≤6 inches off the floor so it trips before hitting a pet or child). Watch for the failure signs that mean call a pro: grinding/popping, the opener running but the door not moving (broken spring — stop using), frayed lift cables, or a visible gap in the torsion coil. ## Full procedure

> Hands-on tasks live in [[ACTION-ITEMS]].

## The two-track model (like HVAC)
| Homeowner track (you) | Pro track (strictly) |
|---|---|
| Lubricate moving parts | **Any spring work** — torsion or extension |
| Clean + inspect tracks | Cable replacement |
| Test auto-reverse + photo eye | Track realignment / re-leveling |
| Balance test (diagnosis only) | Opener logic board / motor repair |
| Tighten loose bolts/brackets | Section/panel replacement |

> ⚠️ **Never touch the springs.** [[GLOSSARY#Torsion spring|Torsion]] (above door) or [[GLOSSARY#Extension spring|extension]] (along tracks) store enough energy to injure or kill. You can *look* to diagnose (balance test) — adjustment/replacement is 100% pro.

## Part 1 — Lubrication (every 3–6 months)
Highest-leverage homeowner task. 10 min. Reduces noise, opener strain, part wear.

### Lubricate
- **Hinges** — pivot points between panels
- **Roller stems** — metal stem in the hinge (NOT the wheel)
- **Springs** — light surface coat (you're spraying the surface, not touching/adjusting)
- **Opener drive** — chain or screw (belt drives usually don't need it)

### Do NOT lubricate
> ⚠️ **The tracks.** Vertical + horizontal tracks should be clean + dry. Lube there collects grit → grinding paste → wears rollers *faster*. Wipe with cloth, don't spray.

### What to use
- Garage-door-specific lube (white lithium grease spray or dedicated silicone). ~$8–12.
- **Avoid WD-40** (it's a solvent, not a lubricant — cleans then dries out).
- Spray, wipe excess, run a full cycle to work lube into pivots.

## Part 2 — Track cleaning + bolt check
- Wipe tracks with damp cloth (no lube). Check for dents.
- Hand-tighten loose brackets/bolts (vibration loosens them). Don't crank.
- Center ceiling bracket where opener hangs — if pulling loose, pro call.
- **Look at lift cables** (springs → bottom brackets). Fraying/rust = pro replacement. Never touch — under spring tension.

## Part 3 — Safety tests (monthly, ~1 min each)
Federal-mandated on all openers since 1993.

### Auto-reverse (mechanical)
1. Place 2x4 flat on floor under center of opening.
2. Close door.
3. Door should strike board and reverse immediately.
4. Doesn't reverse? Adjust "close force" dial on opener per manual. Still fails → service/replace.

### Photo-Eye (beam sensor)
1. Open door. Find two [[GLOSSARY#Photo eye (photoelectric sensor)|sensors]] near floor, each side of opening.
2. Close door. Wave object (broomstick, leg) through beam.
3. Door should reverse immediately.
4. Doesn't? Clean lenses (dust/cobwebs), check alignment (both face each other directly), re-test. Still failing → faulty/mis-wired.

> ⚠️ Photo-eyes must be **≤6 inches off floor**. Higher = door could close on pet/child before beam breaks. Have pro re-locate if higher.

## Part 4 — Balance test (diagnose springs safely)
The one spring-related thing you *can* do — diagnosis only, no adjustment.

1. Close door. Pull [[GLOSSARY#Emergency release cord|red release cord]] (disconnects door from opener).
2. Lift door manually to chest height (halfway).
3. Let go.
   - **Holds position** = balanced
   - **Falls closed or springs open** = springs out of balance
4. Reconnect: pull cord toward motor, run full cycle.

> ⚠️ **Out-of-balance = call pro.** Opener strains + burns out faster on unbalanced door. Spring adjustment strictly pro-only — severe injury risk.

## Part 5 — Signs the door needs a pro
- Loud grinding/popping (worn rollers, failing bearings, spring issue)
- Door jerks or catches (track misalignment, bad roller, worn hinge)
- Opener runs, door doesn't move (**broken spring** — door too heavy; stop using)
- Frayed/rusted lift cables (snap suddenly — pro)
- Visible gap in torsion spring coil (broken — pro immediately)
- Sagging or bent sections (structural)

> ⚠️ **Broken torsion spring** = visible gap in coil. Snapped under tension into two pieces. Door extremely heavy manually, opener can't move it. Stop, don't force, call pro.

## Cadence
| Frequency | Task |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Auto-reverse test; photo-eye test |
| Every 3–6 mo | Lubricate hinges/rollers/springs/opener; wipe tracks; balance test; hand-tighten brackets |
| As needed | Pro: out-of-balance springs, broken spring, frayed cables, opener failing safety tests |
