## The logic
Two heavy-use seasons (cooling, heating) → tune up the system before each one. Homeowner preps before the load hits; books the pro tune-up 2–4 weeks before the season.

## The whole year

### Always (year-round, ~2 min/month)
- [ ] Inspect [[GLOSSARY#Air filter|air filter]] monthly — replace on dirt
- [ ] Keep supply & return vents unobstructed
- [ ] Listen for new sounds, watch for new smells

### Spring — before cooling season (~45 min)
- [ ] Replace air filter
- [ ] Flush condensate drain (vinegar + water)
- [ ] Clear debris 2 ft around outdoor unit
- [ ] Hose off outdoor fins (gently)
- [ ] Check & straighten bent fins
- [ ] Confirm condenser pad is level
- [ ] Switch thermostat to COOL; verify cold air at registers

### Fall — before heating season (~30 min)
- [ ] Replace air filter
- [ ] Clear leaves/debris from outdoor unit (esp. if heat pump)
- [ ] Flush condensate drain (heat pumps produce condensate both modes)
- [ ] Switch thermostat to HEAT; verify warm air at registers
- [ ] Test thermostat setpoint vs. actual delivery
- [ ] If gas heat: **do not** DIY the burner — schedule pro tune-up

### Pro tune-up (twice yearly)
- [ ] Spring: cooling-side tune-up
- [ ] Fall: heating-side tune-up

Books 2–4 weeks before the season. Covers: electrical connections, refrigerant, coil deep-clean, combustion + heat exchanger (gas), system controls. See [[HVAC-preventative-maintenance|the HVAC reference]].

## Outdoor condenser care (the new skill)

### Clean the fins (gently)
1. **Power off** at the outdoor disconnect box (wall near unit) AND breaker. Capacitors hold a charge.
2. Remove top grille/louvered panels — note the fan may be attached to the grille; don't stress the wires.
3. Spray fins from **inside out** with a garden hose on gentle spray. Push dirt back out the way it came in.
4. Stubborn grime: foaming no-rinse coil cleaner, then rinse gently.

> ⚠️ **Never use a pressure washer** — thin aluminum [[GLOSSARY#Fins|fins]] will shred. Garden hose + standard nozzle is the maximum.

### Straighten bent fins
Use a [[GLOSSARY#Fin comb (fin straightener)|fin comb]] (~$10) sized to the coil's fins-per-inch (home coils usually 8–14 FPI).
1. Spray fins with soapy water for lubrication.
2. Engage several teeth in **straight** fins next to the bent section.
3. Draw comb gently up/down through the bent area — a few fins at a time.

Crushed-over-large-area fins = pro job or coil replacement.

### Check the pad
[[GLOSSARY#Condenser pad|Condenser pad]] should be level. Frost heave/settling can tilt it over years → strains compressor. Noticeably tilted = call a pro to re-level.

## For your unit: Carrier 24SPA630W003
Sources: [Carrier 24SPA6 product data](https://www.shareddocs.com/hvac/docs/1009/Public/0D/24SPA6-04PD.pdf) · [Carrier 24-25 series service manual](https://www.shareddocs.com/hvac/docs/1009/Public/07/24-25-2SM.pdf). Confirmed specs: **25 FPI** outdoor coil, louvered WeatherArmor Ultra steel coil guard, direct-drive propeller fan (¼ HP, 1100 RPM), Puron (R-410A), 2.5 ton / 30,000 Btuh, 16 SEER2.

> **Note:** the 24SPA is an **AC-only condenser**, not a heat pump. Heating is a separate system (likely a furnace) — confirm and build the fall checklist around it.

### The cabinet advantage
WeatherArmor Ultra = louvered steel panel protecting the coil. Basic fin cleaning needs **no removal** — hose through the louvers. Grille/fan removal only for deep coil clean or motor service.

### Tools for grille & fan-motor removal
| Tool | Use |
|------|-----|
| **5/16" nut driver** (or 5/16" hex socket + driver, ~6" reach) | Top grille & side panel screws (all 5/16" hex-head sheet-metal) |
| **Phillips #2 bit** | Backup for interior screws (motor mount, control cover) |
| **Adjustable wrench or 7/16" socket** | Fan blade set screw / hub nut (only if separating blade from shaft) |
| **Second person** | Top grille = fan motor mount; motor hangs from cover, has wiring. Awkward solo. |
| **Needle-nose pliers / small flathead** | Motor wiring harness disconnect at control board |
| **Zip-top bag** | Screws are a specific self-tapping 5/16" hex — don't lose them |

### Grille removal procedure (your unit)
1. **Power off two ways:** pull disconnect block (outdoor box) AND flip condenser breaker at the panel. Verify dead with non-contact voltage tester — run capacitor holds a charge.
2. Remove perimeter top grille screws with 5/16" nut driver.
3. Lift top grille slowly — **fan motor + blade are bolted to the underside**; they come up with the grille. Wiring harness runs motor → control board.
4. Support grille before wires go taut. Options:
   - *Light clean:* prop grille up without stressing wires, clean coil from top, don't fully disconnect.
   - *Full removal:* unplug motor harness from control board, lift grille + motor + fan assembly clear, set down gently on protected surface.
5. Clean coil inside-out with garden hose (gentle). Vacuum loose debris from cabinet base.
6. Reassembly: reverse. Reconnect harness if removed, lower grille, reinstall perimeter screws **hand-tight** (sheet-metal — overtightening strips them).
7. Restore power, verify fan runs.

> ⚠️ **Do not loosen the fan blade set screw** unless deliberately removing the blade. If the blade shifts on the shaft it can contact the fan orifice or coil guard and damage the unit. For coil cleaning only, leave blade attached to motor.

### Your fin comb: 25 FPI
Coil is **25 fins per inch** — finer than the old "8–14 FPI" rule of thumb.
- Need a comb that specifically includes **25 FPI**. Most multi-sided combs top out at ~20 FPI — **check packaging before buying.**
- Search "25 FPI fin comb" or "fin comb 25" — usually under $15. Newer "soft comb" / evaporator-style flexible combs cover the fine 25 FPI spacing.
- Procedure unchanged: soapy water lubrication, engage teeth in straight fins beside the bend, a few fins at a time. At 25 FPI fins are closely spaced and delicate — go slow.

> ⚠️ **Refrigerant is pro-only.** Unit uses Puron (R-410A). Never open, crimp, or heat the copper refrigerant lines, or touch service valves. Exterior coil + cabinet cleaning only. Refrigerant work requires EPA certification.

## Heat pump owners (not your system)
*Your Carrier 24SPA is AC-only — this section doesn't apply. Kept for reference.*
- Outdoor unit runs in winter too (heating mode) — keep it clear of snow/ice year-round.
- Condensate drain matters in both seasons — fall flush as important as spring.
- Light frosting of the outdoor coil in cold humid weather is **normal**. The unit defrosts itself periodically. Don't chip, hose, or call a pro unless it stays heavily iced for hours.

## DIY or pro?
**DIY:** filter, drain flush, debris clearing, gentle fin cleaning, fin combing, visual inspections, thermostat settings.
**Pro:** refrigerant, electrical inside equipment, gas/combustion, coil disassembly, pad re-leveling, any diagnosis (uneven temps, short cycling, strange sounds).
