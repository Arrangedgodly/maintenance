# HVAC Preventative Maintenance — Reference

The compressed reference for keeping an HVAC system healthy. This is the doc you'll come back to. For term definitions, see [[GLOSSARY]].

> Grounded in the [ENERGY STAR Maintenance Checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) and the [EPA Guide to Energy-Efficient Heating and Cooling](https://www.energystar.gov/sites/default/files/asset/bank/hvac_tips.pdf).

---

## The two maintenance tracks

HVAC maintenance splits cleanly into **two tracks**. Know which one a task belongs to before starting:

| Track | Who | Tasks |
|-------|-----|-------|
| **Homeowner track** | You | Filter changes, clearing debris, visual checks, thermostat battery |
| **Pro tune-up track** | Licensed tech | Electrical, refrigerant, coils, gas, system controls |

The single biggest mistake homeowners make is either (a) skipping the cheap homeowner tasks until something breaks, or (b) attempting the pro tasks and voiding warranties / creating safety hazards.

---

## Homeowner track — the maintenance cadence

### Monthly
- **Inspect the [[GLOSSARY#Air filter|air filter]].** Hold it up to a light — if you can't see light through it, replace it. See [[lessons/0001-hvac-filter-change]] for the full procedure.
- **Check the thermostat.** Confirm settings match the season. Replace batteries if it's battery-powered.

### Every 1–3 months (depends on filter + household)
- **Replace the air filter.** Faster cadence if: pets, allergies, remodeling dust, or MERV 11+ filters (they catch more, so they load up faster).

### Seasonally (spring & fall — before the heavy season)
- **Clear debris around the outdoor [[GLOSSARY#Condenser coil|condenser]] unit.** Trim plants back 2 ft minimum. Hose off the exterior fins gently (no pressure washer).
- **Clear the [[GLOSSARY#Condensate drain|condensate drain line]].** A clog causes water backups and shutdowns. (Covered in a later lesson.)
- **Visually inspect** supply/return vents — make sure furniture or drapes aren't blocking them.

### Annually
- **Schedule a professional tune-up.** ENERGY STAR recommends twice-yearly (spring for cooling, fall for heating). At minimum, once a year before the heavy-use season.

## Pro tune-up track — what a tune-up covers

When you pay for a tune-up, you're buying this checklist (from ENERGY STAR):

- [ ] Check and calibrate **thermostat** settings
- [ ] **Tighten all electrical connections** and measure voltage/current on motors
- [ ] **Lubricate** all moving parts (friction = wear + energy waste)
- [ ] Inspect and clear the **condensate drain**
- [ ] Check **system controls** (correct startup, operation, shutdown sequence)
- [ ] (Cooling) Clean [[GLOSSARY#Evaporator coil|evaporator]] and [[GLOSSARY#Condenser coil|condenser]] coils; check refrigerant level and adjust
- [ ] (Heating) Inspect gas/fuel connections, heat exchanger, and burner combustion

> **Why this matters:** A tune-up catches the things that quietly cost money — low refrigerant, dirty coils, a cracked heat exchanger — and the things that are safety-critical, like a gas leak or bad combustion.

## The DIY / Pro boundary

**DIY-safe:**
- Air filter changes
- Clearing debris from the outdoor unit
- Cleaning the exterior of fins (gently)
- Flushing the condensate drain line
- Thermostat settings + batteries
- Visual inspections

**Call a pro:**
- Anything involving **[[GLOSSARY#Refrigerant|refrigerant]]** (EPA certification required by law)
- **Electrical** inside the equipment (capacitors hold a charge even when powered off — dangerous)
- **Gas** connections or combustion
- **Coil deep-clean** requiring disassembly
- Diagnosing uneven heating/cooling, short cycling, or strange sounds
- Anything still under warranty (DIY work may void it)

Sources: [West Air Heating — DIY vs Pro](https://www.westairheating.com/diy-hvac-tips-simple-maintenance-and-when-to-call-a-pro), [Phil's Heating — HVAC Electrical](https://www.philsheatingandair.com/diy-vs-pro-when-to-call-a-technician-for-hvac-electrical-issues)

## Red flags — call a pro now, not at the next tune-up

Stop reading and call if you notice any of these:
- **Warm air** from vents when set to cool (or cold when set to heat)
- **Short cycling** — system turns on and off rapidly
- **Burning or electrical smell**
- **Hissing** near refrigerant lines (possible leak)
- **Water pooling** near the indoor unit (clogged or broken condensate path)
- **Sudden spike** in energy bills with no usage change
- **Ice** on the outdoor unit or refrigerant lines (cooling mode)
