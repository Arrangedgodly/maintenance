# Garbage Disposer

> A misunderstood appliance — once you know there are no blades, the rest follows.

## Summary
The key mental model: disposers have **no blades** — the [[GLOSSARY#Flywheel (disposer)|flywheel]] spins, [[GLOSSARY#Impellers (disposer)|impellers]] fling food outward by centrifugal force against the stationary [[GLOSSARY#Grind ring (shredder ring)|grind ring]], and water flushes the particles down. Nothing to sharpen ("ice sharpens blades" is a myth; ice does clean). So hard items (bones, pits, shells), fibrous veg (celery, corn husks), starchy peels, and — critically — [[GLOSSARY#FOG (fats, oils, grease)|FOG]] all defeat it (same no-FOG rule as [[Drain & Sewer Clog Prevention|sewer clog prevention]]). Always run **cold** water (not hot) — cold keeps grease solid so it grinds and flushes; hot liquefies it to coat the drain and re-solidify downstream. Two fixes every homeowner should know: **hum-and-no-spin** = jammed flywheel, freed with a ¼" Allen wrench in the underside [[GLOSSARY#Hex socket (disposer)|hex socket]]; **dead motor** = the red [[GLOSSARY#Reset button (disposer)|reset button]] popped out — clear any jam, let it cool, press it. Silent (no hum) = no power (check reset, plug, breaker). If it drains a [[Dishwasher Maintenance|dishwasher]], the knockout plug must be out and the [[GLOSSARY#High-loop / air gap (dishwasher drain to disposer)|high loop]] present. Never pour chemical drain cleaner in a disposer. At ~8–15 yr lifespan and ~$100–200 per unit, diagnosis past the basic fixes rarely pays — replace.

## The myth: there are no blades
Disposers grind by **centrifugal force + impact**, not cutting.
- [[GLOSSARY#Flywheel (disposer)|Flywheel]] spins → [[GLOSSARY#Impellers (disposer)|impellers]] (lugs) throw food outward
- Food hits stationary [[GLOSSARY#Grind ring (shredder ring)|grind ring]] → ground between ring + flywheel
- Water flushes particles down

→ Nothing to sharpen. "Ice sharpens blades" is a myth (ice does clean — see below). Hard items (bones) and stringy items (celery) defeat it because there's no cutting edge.

## Part 1 — What NOT to put down
| Item | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| [[GLOSSARY#FOG (fats, oils, grease)|Grease/fat/oil]] | Solidifies in chamber + drain; coats + clogs. Jar it, trash it. (Same rule as [[Drain & Sewer Clog Prevention|drain/sewer clog prevention]].) |
| Bones, fruit pits, seafood shells | Too hard for grind ring; jam flywheel or damage ring |
| Fibrous veg (celery, corn husks, onion skins, asparagus, artichoke) | Strings tangle around flywheel + impellers → jam |
| Potato peels | Starch → paste → coats chamber + clogs drain |
| Pasta, rice | Expand in water even after grinding → paste clog downstream |
| Coffee grounds | Settle + pack like sediment in pipe |
| Eggshells | Membrane wraps impellers; shell grit packs drain |
| Non-food (utensils, glass) | #1 cause of sudden jam — always check before running |

> ⚠️ **Always run COLD water (not hot)** — before, during, and 10–15s after. Cold keeps grease solid → ground + flushed as particles. Hot liquefies grease → coats drain line → re-solidifies downstream → clog. Counterintuitive but important.

## Part 2 — The two fixes every homeowner should know

### Fix 1 — Hum-and-no-spin (jammed flywheel)
Motor gets power but flywheel stuck (bone/fork/fibrous wad). Don't keep running — overheats + trips reset.
1. Switch off. Unplug under sink OR breaker. Confirm dead.
2. Flashlight into chamber. Remove visible object with tongs/pliers (**never your hand**).
3. Find [[GLOSSARY#Hex socket (disposer)|hex socket]] in center of underside.
4. Insert ¼" Allen wrench (usually clipped to side of unit). Rock back + forth until flywheel breaks free + spins smooth.
5. No wrench? Broom handle from above (unit off) can lever flywheel free. Less precise.
6. Restore power. Cold water. Test — spins freely.

### Fix 2 — Reset button (tripped motor)
Red [[GLOSSARY#Reset button (disposer)|reset button]] on bottom pops out when motor overheats. After clearing any jam:
1. Let motor cool 5–10 min if hot.
2. Press red button firmly → clicks, stays depressed.
3. Cold water. Test.

> ⚠️ **Won't stay in / trips immediately?** Jam not fully cleared, or motor failed. Re-clear jam (hex wrench). Still trips → motor dead → replacement (~$100–200, DIY swap).

### Silent (no hum) = no power
Check: (1) reset pushed in, (2) plugged in, (3) breaker. All OK + silent → switch or motor failed (multimeter for switch; dead motor = replace).

## Part 3 — Cleaning + odor prevention
Chamber biofilm of food residue smells, esp. after sitting unused.

### Ice flush (monthly)
- 2 cups ice cubes into chamber (+ 1 tbsp baking soda optional)
- Cold water + run disposer until ice ground + flushed
- Knocks loose residue from impellers + grind ring

### Citrus freshen (as needed)
- Cut-up lemon/lime/orange peels. Grind with cold water. Citrus oil freshens chamber + drain.

### Deep clean (persistent odor)
1. Power off (unplug/breaker).
2. Scrub under rubber baffle at sink opening — worst gunk/slime hides here. Small brush + soapy water.
3. Baking soda (½ cup) + white vinegar (1 cup). Fizz 10 min. Flush cold + run.

> ⚠️ **Never chemical drain cleaners** (Drano etc.) in disposer. Damage rubber + sink seal. Hazard for whoever opens/augers later ([[Drain & Sewer Clog Prevention|drain/sewer clog prevention]]).

## Part 4 — Baffle + dishwasher connection

### Rubber baffle (splash guard)
Flaps at sink opening prevent splashback. Get slimy/crack/curl inward → food splashback. Curled/stiff → flip or replace (~$5, snaps in).

### Dishwasher drains into disposer
- **Knockout plug** — small metal plug in disposer's dishwasher port must be knocked out from inside at install. Installer forgot → dishwasher can't drain at all. (Symptom: DW won't drain after new disposer install.)
- **Run disposer before starting dishwasher** — clogged disposer backs up into DW drain line.

## Part 5 — When to replace (~8–15 yr lifespan)
Cheap enough (~$100–200 homeowner unit) that diagnosis past basic fixes rarely pays. Replace if:
- Frequent jams even with careful use (worn grind ring)
- Persistent odor surviving deep clean (chamber corrosion)
- Water leaking from bottom of unit (failed internal seal → motor housing corroded through)
- Motor hums, flywheel spins freely, reset works, but won't run under power (failed capacitor/motor)
- 12+ yr old + any of above

## Cadence
| Frequency | Task |
|---|---|
| Each use | Check chamber first; cold water; keep running 10–15s after |
| Monthly | Ice flush (+ baking soda); citrus freshen if smelly |
| As needed | Deep clean; jam clear (hex wrench); reset button |
| 8–15 yr | Replace: frequent jams / persistent odor / bottom leak / dead motor |
