# HVAC Filter Change

> The single highest-leverage [[GLOSSARY#HVAC|HVAC]] maintenance task a homeowner owns: swapping the [[GLOSSARY#Air filter|air filter]] so the system doesn't choke.

## Summary
- A clogged [[GLOSSARY#Air filter|air filter]] restricts [[GLOSSARY#Return air|return air]], forcing the [[GLOSSARY#Blower|blower]] to work harder and risking a frozen [[GLOSSARY#Evaporator coil|evaporator coil]] or an overheated heat exchanger. It is the #1 EPA/ENERGY STAR homeowner task.
- The filter lives on the [[GLOSSARY#Return air|return-air]] side — either behind a return grille or in the blower compartment. Use only one; two in series double the resistance.
- Size by the **nominal** dimensions printed on the cardboard edge (e.g. `20×25×1`); thickness is the critical dimension.
- Choose a [[GLOSSARY#MERV rating|MERV rating]] for your needs — **higher ≠ better**. MERV 8/11 is the safe default; MERV 13 can choke an older system like a dirty filter would.
- Install with the airflow arrow pointing toward the equipment ([[GLOSSARY#Supply air|supply air]] direction).
- Cadence: **check monthly** with the light test, replace on dirt (not the calendar) — typically 1–3 months. Pets, allergies, wildfire smoke, and higher MERV all shorten the interval.

## The core idea
The [[GLOSSARY#Air filter|air filter]] traps dust before air reaches the equipment. A clogged filter chokes the system, wastes energy, shortens equipment life, and can freeze the [[GLOSSARY#Evaporator coil|evaporator coil]] or overheat the heat exchanger. It's the #1 homeowner maintenance task per EPA / ENERGY STAR.

## Where it lives
The filter is on the [[GLOSSARY#Return air|return air]] side — either:
- Behind a **return grille** (wall/ceiling), or
- In the **blower compartment** of the indoor unit.

If a home has both locations, use only one filter (at the return grille). Two in series double the resistance.

## Sizing
- Nominal size printed on the cardboard edge: e.g. `20×25×1` (W × H × thickness, inches).
- **Thickness is the critical dimension** — the slot accepts one thickness.
- Nominal > actual measurement (a "20×25×1" really measures ~19½ × 24½ × ¾"). Buy the nominal size.

## Choosing MERV
See [[GLOSSARY#MERV rating|MERV rating]].

| MERV | Catches | Default for |
|------|---------|-------------|
| 8 | Dust, pollen, lint | Most homes — safe default |
| 11 | + pet dander, fine dust | Pets, mild allergies |
| 13 | + bacteria, smoke | Allergies, smoke-season (only if system handles it) |

**Higher ≠ better.** MERV 13 on an older/smaller system can restrict airflow enough to cause the same damage as a clogged filter. Default to 8 or 11 unless the system is confirmed to handle higher.

## Airflow direction
- Arrow on the edge points **toward the equipment** (the [[GLOSSARY#Blower|blower]]), in the direction of airflow.
- Return grille: arrow points into the ductwork. Equipment slot: arrow points toward the blower.
- When unsure, photograph the old filter before removing it.

## Replacement cadence
- **Check monthly** via the hold-up-to-light test. Replace when you can't see light clearly through the media.
- Most homes: **1–3 months**. Replace on dirt, not on the calendar.
- Shortens the interval: pets, allergies, construction dust, higher MERV, more occupants, busy roads, wildfire smoke.

## Swap procedure
1. Thermostat OFF.
2. Remove old filter.
3. Light-test the old filter — that's your interval data.
4. Install new filter, arrow toward the equipment.
5. Close up. Thermostat back to setting.

## DIY or pro?
**DIY.** This is squarely homeowner-track — the highest-leverage task you fully own.
