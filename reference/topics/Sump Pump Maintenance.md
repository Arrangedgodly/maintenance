# Sump Pump Maintenance

> The [[GLOSSARY#Sump pump|sump pump]] is the last line of defense between groundwater and the basement floor — and the most common single point of failure when a basement floods.

## Summary
Mental model: two failure modes — **power loss** (storm kills electricity) and **pump death** (motor burns out, [[GLOSSARY#Float switch|float sticks]], impeller jams). A generator covers only power loss; a [[GLOSSARY#Battery backup sump pump|battery backup]] is a second pump in the pit, so it covers both. The core homeowner task is a 5-minute **quarterly pour-water test**: fill the [[GLOSSARY#Sump pit (sump basin)|sump pit]], watch the [[GLOSSARY#Float switch|float switch]] rise and trigger the pump, confirm smooth hum and clean shut-off, confirm water exits the [[GLOSSARY#Discharge line|discharge line]] 10–20 ft from the foundation. The [[GLOSSARY#Check valve|check valve]] on the discharge stops backflow (loud thunk every cycle = failing). Backup options trade off: [[GLOSSARY#Battery backup sump pump|battery]] (~6–12 hr runtime, ~$800–2,200), [[GLOSSARY#Water-powered backup|water-powered]] (unlimited runtime on municipal water, ~$500–1,200), or generator (whole-home). Pumps last ~7–10 yr and cost only ~$150–300 — replace proactively at year 8–9, not on failure. The discharge line must drain fully before winter or a frozen line burns out the pump. First step: confirm you actually have one (look for a ~18–24" covered pit in the lowest floor).

## Full procedure

> Hands-on tasks live in [[ACTION-ITEMS]].

> **First, confirm you have one.** Look for a covered circular pit (~18–24") in the lowest part of the basement floor. If absent and basement is dry, informational only. If basement is damp/wet and no pump exists → talk to a waterproofing pro about installing one.

## The mental model: two failure modes
| | Power loss | Pump death |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Storm knocks out electricity | Motor burns out, float sticks, impeller jams |
| Covered by | Battery backup, water-powered backup, generator | Battery backup (it's a second pump) |
| NOT covered by | — | **Generator** (dead pump is dead — generator only supplies power) |

→ A generator isn't a complete sump backup. A [[GLOSSARY#Battery backup sump pump|battery backup]] is a second pump in the pit, so it covers both.

## Part 1 — The quarterly pour-water test (core homeowner task)
5 minutes, every 3 months + after heavy storms.
1. Remove pit cover. Note float, discharge pipe, cords — don't disturb.
2. **Slowly pour 5 gal water into pit.** Don't dump — watch float rise.
3. At trigger height, pump starts. Listen for smooth hum (no grinding/rattling).
4. Pit empties to off-level quickly; pump shuts off cleanly (no run-on, no short-cycle).
5. **Confirm water exits outside** (exterior outlet clear, not blocked/frozen).
6. Replace cover (keeps debris out; sealed cover blocks humidity + radon).

> **Pump doesn't start?** Check: (1) plugged in? (2) breaker tripped? (3) float tangled/stuck on debris? Free float, re-test. Still nothing → float switch or motor failed → replace pump (~$150–300, straightforward homeowner swap).

### Float-lift shortcut (between quarterly pours)
Lift float manually 2–3 sec, confirm motor starts, lower immediately. Tests motor + switch but not pumping action or discharge path. Do the pour-water at least annually; float-lift is fine in-between.

## Part 2 — Backup systems

| | Battery backup | [[GLOSSARY#Water-powered backup|Water-powered]] | Generator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power loss | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Pump death | ✓ (second pump) | ✓ (second pump) | ✗ |
| Runtime | ~6–12 hr | Unlimited (city water) | Days (w/ fuel) |
| Requires | Battery replace every 3–5 yr | Municipal water only | Fuel, manual setup (portable) |
| Cost installed | $800–2,200 | $500–1,200 | $500–10,000+ |

> **Pragmatic pick:** battery backup covers both failure modes, activates automatically, second pump in pit. Best sump-specific investment for most homes. Generator only if you want whole-home backup anyway.

### Backup test (quarterly)
1. Unplug primary (simulates failure).
2. Pour water until backup float activates.
3. Backup starts + drains pit.
4. Battery: check indicator light (green = charged) + multimeter voltage (~12.6V+ full; <12.0V = charge/replace).
5. Reconnect primary.

## Part 3 — The discharge line (where the water goes)
- **Terminate 10–20 ft from foundation**, downhill, away from house. Dumping at base → water returns to footing drains → infinite loop → pump burns out.
- **Exterior outlet clear** of debris, soil, vegetation. Blocked outlet backs up line → burns out pump.
- **Winter freeze:** line must slope so water drains completely — no low spots where water pools + freezes. Frozen January line burns out pump. Buried discharge below frost depth if prone.
- **[[GLOSSARY#Check valve|Check valve]]** on discharge just above pump prevents backflow. Loud "thunk" every cycle = failing or wrong type. Confirm arrow points away from pit.

## Part 4 — Annual pit cleaning
1. Unplug pump.
2. Remove pump from pit (disconnect discharge union — towel ready).
3. Clean sediment/gravel/debris (wet/dry vac). No chemicals.
4. Inspect pump intake screen — clean clogs (sediment here jams impeller).
5. Reinstall, reconnect, pour-water test.

## Part 5 — Failure signs (replace proactively)
Pumps last ~7–10 yr. Cheap enough (~$150–300) that waiting for failure is a bad bet — replace proactively at year 8–9.
- Unusual noises (grinding, rattling, straining — worn bearings, debris)
- Runs continuously (stuck switch, failed check valve, undersized)
- Short-cycles (check valve failing, pit too small)
- Visible rust (pump body or discharge)
- Vibration/shaking (misaligned/damaged impeller)
- **7+ years old** — replace on age, not on failure
- Infrequent operation — rarely runs may seize up (quarterly test catches this)

## Cadence
| Frequency | Task |
|---|---|
| Quarterly | Pour-water primary test; backup test; battery voltage; confirm exterior discharge clear |
| Annually | Pull pump + clean pit/screen; inspect discharge for slope + freeze risk; replace pump at 7+ yr |
| Every 3–5 yr | Replace backup battery (even if tests OK) |
| Before winter | Confirm discharge line drains fully (no freeze-prone low spots); outlet above snow line |
