# Toilet Repair

> The capstone plumbing lesson: diagnosing the running/leaking toilet from symptoms, plus the three common repairs — flapper, fill valve, wax ring. Connects the dye test in [[Finding Leaks Early|finding leaks]] to the culprit.

## Summary
Four parts live in the tank: the [[GLOSSARY#Fill valve (toilet)|fill valve]] (tall, left, refills and shuts off at a set level), the [[GLOSSARY#Flush valve (toilet)|flush valve]] (center, overflow tube + seat), the [[GLOSSARY#Flapper (toilet)|flapper]] (rubber seal over the seat — the #1 cause of running toilets), and the handle + [[GLOSSARY#Flapper chain|chain]]. The one-glance diagnosis: water spilling down the [[GLOSSARY#Overflow tube|overflow tube]] means the **fill valve**; water below the overflow but still trickling into the bowl means the **flapper** (confirm with a dye test).

The flapper swap is the most common toilet repair (~$5–10, 10 min) — take the old one to the store, wipe the flush-valve seat, and set the chain to ¼–½" slack (too tight trickles, too loose won't seal). The fill valve (~$10, Fluidmaster-style cup float) must sit with its critical-level mark 1" above the overflow tube and must have its refill tube connected or the bowl won't refill. Both rely on the [[GLOSSARY#Shut-off valve (angle stop / stop valve)|fixture shut-off]] behind the toilet working — exercise it before you need it.

A [[GLOSSARY#Wax ring (toilet)|wax ring]] fails when water seeps out the base only on flush, or when loose [[GLOSSARY#Closet bolts|closet bolts]] or a broken [[GLOSSARY#Floor flange (closet flange)|floor flange]] cause a wobble. Lift straight up, scrape all old wax, never reuse a ring, and hand-tighten + ¼ turn (over-tightening cracks the base). A failed flange is the real, bigger problem hiding behind a recurring wax-ring leak. Track repairs in [[ACTION-ITEMS]] and feed dye-test results into [[Finding Leaks Early|finding leaks]].

## Tank anatomy (4 things in the tank)
- **[[GLOSSARY#Fill valve (toilet)|Fill valve]]** — tall, left. Refills tank, includes float. Shuts off at set level.
- **[[GLOSSARY#Flush valve (toilet)|Flush valve]]** — center. Overflow tube + seat. Releases water to bowl on flush.
- **[[GLOSSARY#Flapper (toilet)|Flapper]]** — over flush valve seat. Rubber seal lifted by handle. #1 cause of running toilets.
- **Handle + [[GLOSSARY#Flapper chain|chain]]** — left, to flapper. Chain length critical.

**Flow:** fill valve → tank. Flush → handle → chain lifts flapper → water rushes past seat → bowl. Tank refills → float shuts fill valve off.

## Diagnosis flow (read first)

| Symptom | Failing part | Confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Trickling into bowl; tank refills every few min | **Flapper** or corroded flush valve seat | Dye test — color in bowl in 15 min |
| Running continuously; sound from fill valve | **Fill valve** not shutting off | Water level at/above overflow tube |
| Water spilling down [[GLOSSARY#Overflow tube|overflow tube]] | **Fill valve / float** set too high or failed | Water line above overflow top |
| Weak flush; handle loose, flapper barely lifts | **Chain too loose** or broken handle lever | Watch chain on flush |
| Flapper lifts, drops instantly — incomplete flush | **Chain too tight** | ¼–½" slack at rest is correct |
| Water at base only when flushed | **[[GLOSSARY#Wax ring (toilet)|Wax ring]]** | Wipe base dry, flush, watch seam |
| Toilet wobbles | Loose [[GLOSSARY#Closet bolts|closet bolts]] or broken [[GLOSSARY#Floor flange (closet flange)|flange]] | Pop caps; tighten. Spinning bolts = flange |

> **One-glance test:** water spilling into overflow = **fill valve**. Water below overflow but still running into bowl = **flapper**.

## Repair 1 — Flapper (#1 toilet repair, ~10 min, ~$5–10)
Rubber degrades → cracks, warps, mineral-fouls → silent 24/7 leak (the dye-test culprit).
1. Shut off water behind toilet. Flush, sponge out tank.
2. Unhook chain from handle lever.
3. Remove old flapper (snaps off pegs or slides up).
4. **Take old flapper to store** — dozens of sizes, match by eye unreliable.
5. **Wipe flush valve seat** (mineral crust/nick causes same symptom — new flapper won't seal on rough seat).
6. Snap new flapper on. Reattach chain with ¼–½" slack.
7. Water on. Flush-test — lifts fully, drops clean, no trickle.

> ⚠️ **New flapper still leaks?** Flush valve seat is rough/pitted/cracked. Slip-on seat repair kit (~$8) resurfaces seat, or replace flush valve (bigger job).

### Chain adjustment (most common DIY mistake)
- **Too tight** → flapper pulled off seat → constant trickle. Loosen a link.
- **Too loose** → slack caught under flapper → won't seal; handle has dead travel. Tighten a link.
- **Right:** ¼–½" slack at rest; lifts fully on flush, drops clean.

## Repair 2 — Fill valve (~15–20 min, ~$10)
Modern Fluidmaster-style cup-float valve fits virtually every toilet.
1. Shut off water, flush, sponge tank. Disconnect supply line (compression nut — towel ready).
2. Unscrew lock nut (outside bottom of tank). Remove old valve.
3. **Adjust new valve height** — critical level (CL) mark must sit 1" above overflow tube's top.
4. Insert valve, hand-tighten + ½ turn pliers. **Don't overtighten (crack the tank).**
5. **Connect refill tube** from fill valve to top of overflow tube (refills the bowl — without it, weak flush + sewer gas).
6. Reconnect supply line. Water on slowly. Check for leaks.
7. Adjust float so water fills to ~½" below overflow tube top.
8. Flush-test — refills, shuts off clean, level below overflow.

> ⚠️ **Two mistakes:** (1) forgetting refill tube → bowl won't refill. (2) float set above overflow → water runs continuously into overflow, valve never shuts off.

## Repair 3 — Wax ring (leaking at base, ~1 hr, ~$5–15)
**Suspect when:** water at base only when flushing, sewer smell, wobble, ceiling damage below.

1. Shut off water, flush, sponge tank + bowl. Disconnect supply line.
2. Pop bolt caps, unscrew closet bolt nuts (hacksaw if corroded/spinning).
3. Score caulk at base (utility knife).
4. **Lift toilet straight up** (heavy ~70–100 lb — get helper). Carry to towel/cardboard (wax smears).
5. **Remove old wax ring** (gloves). Scrape all wax off flange + toilet horn. New ring won't seal over old wax.
6. **Inspect flange** — cracked/corroded/broken = real problem. Wax ring alone won't fix failed flange. Repair plate (~$10) or full flange replacement.
7. Press new wax ring onto flange (room temp — cold ring won't compress evenly).
8. **Lower toilet straight down over bolts. Don't drag sideways** (smears wax, breaks seal). Sit on it to compress.
9. Washers + nuts, alternate tightening, **hand-tight + ¼ turn** (overtightening cracks base).
10. Reconnect water. Caulk base (leave gap at back so future leak shows). Flush-test.

> ⚠️ **Never reuse a wax ring.** Compresses once to exact position. Toilet lifted = ring done = must replace before reseating.

## Quick adjustments (no parts)
- **Float:** wrong water level → adjust screw/clip so water line ~½" below overflow top.
- **Closet bolts:** wobble → pop caps, snug nuts. Won't tighten = flange issue.

## DIY / Pro boundary
| DIY | Call plumber |
|---|---|
| Flapper + chain | Broken floor flange (esp. cast iron) |
| Fill valve | Leak persists after wax ring replacement |
| Handle | Drain pipe below flange |
| Wax ring (comfortable) | Cracked toilet base/tank |
| Float + chain adjustment | Can't diagnose the symptom |
