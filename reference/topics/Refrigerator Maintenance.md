# Refrigerator Maintenance

> The fridge runs 24/7/365 — light, infrequent maintenance directly protects the [[GLOSSARY#Compressor (refrigerator)|compressor]], the expensive part. Covers coils, gasket, water filter, and the ice-maker supply line.

## Summary
A fridge moves heat from inside the box to outside via two coil systems: the self-defrosting [[GLOSSARY#Evaporator coils (refrigerator)|evaporator]] (no cleaning needed) and the dust-collecting [[GLOSSARY#Condenser coils (refrigerator)|condenser]] behind the [[GLOSSARY#Kickplate / toe grille|kickplate]] or back. Dust on the condenser insulates it, so the [[GLOSSARY#Compressor (refrigerator)|compressor]] runs hotter and dies sooner — vacuum the coils (soft brush, no water), cadence driven by pets, dust, and whether the coils are sealed vs. exposed.

Test the [[GLOSSARY#Door gasket (refrigerator)|door gasket]] with the [[GLOSSARY#Dollar-bill test (gasket seal)|dollar-bill test]] — resistance means a good seal, a slip means a gap (clean the groove first, replace only persistent gaps/cracks, ~$30–60 model-specific). Replace the water filter every 6 months and read the cert: [[GLOSSARY#NSF/ANSI 42 vs. 53 (water filters)|NSF 42]] is taste only, [[GLOSSARY#NSF/ANSI 42 vs. 53 (water filters)|NSF 53]] removes lead/cysts — a pre-1986 home needs 53. Flush 2–3 gallons after a swap to clear carbon fines.

The hidden flood risk is the [[GLOSSARY#Ice maker supply line|ice maker supply line]] behind the fridge: swap plastic for braided stainless (same logic as washer hoses), and replace any [[GLOSSARY#Saddle valve (self-piercing valve)|saddle valve]] with a proper tee + ball valve. Refrigerant/sealed-system work is pro-only (EPA cert required). Track every task in [[ACTION-ITEMS]] and on the [[Master Seasonal Checklist|seasonal checklist]] (summer coil clean).

## The mental model
A fridge moves heat from inside the box to outside. Two coil systems:
- **[[GLOSSARY#Evaporator coils (refrigerator)|Evaporator]]** (inside, cold) — self-defrosting, no cleaning
- **[[GLOSSARY#Condenser coils (refrigerator)|Condenser]]** (outside/back/base, warm) — collects dust → **clean this**

Dust on condenser = insulation = [[GLOSSARY#Compressor (refrigerator)|compressor]] runs longer/hotter = shorter life + higher bills.

## Task 1 — Clean condenser coils

### How often (honest answer)
Cadence varies by situation (see table below): exposed rear coils or a dusty home/pets → every ~6 months; sealed/front-accessible condensers in a clean home → every 12–24 months. ([Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/guides/cleaning-your-fridge-coils/): many modern fridges have sealed condensers that collect little dust.)

| Situation | Cadence |
|---|---|
| Pets/dusty home, floor-level fridge against wall | Every 6 months |
| Modern sealed/base condenser, no pets | Every 12–24 months |
| Older fridge (10+ yr) with exposed rear coils | Every 6 months |

**Empirical approach:** pull the kickplate once, look, decide. Visibly dusty → clean; not → check in a year.

### Finding the coils
- Behind the [[GLOSSARY#Kickplate / toe grille|kickplate/toe grille]] at base (front) — most modern
- On the back (older models — big black grid)
- Some modern fridges: sealed inside casing, not serviceable (check manual)

### The clean
1. Unplug fridge / flip breaker.
2. Remove kickplate (snap-off or screws).
3. Vacuum with soft brush attachment — primary tool. Don't bend fins.
4. Refrigerator coil brush (~$8) reaches between coil layers. Sweep, vacuum again.
5. Vacuum floor underneath (dust bunnies get pulled back).
6. Replace kickplate. Restore power.

> ⚠️ Don't bend fins (thin aluminum, brush along the fin direction). No pressure washers, no water spraying — fridge is electrical.

## Task 2 — Test the door gasket

[[GLOSSARY#Door gasket (refrigerator)|Gasket]] failure → cold leaks → compressor runs constantly → food spoils faster.

### Dollar-bill test ([[GLOSSARY#Dollar-bill test (gasket seal)|procedure]])
1. Place dollar bill across gasket, half in/out.
2. Close door on it.
3. Pull slowly. **Resistance = good. Slips easily = gap.**
4. Repeat around each door — top, sides, bottom, esp. hinge side (fails first).

One weak spot ≠ auto-replace. Clean groove (crumbs break seal), check hinge alignment first. Persistent gaps, cracks, hardened gasket → replace.

### Replacement (~$30–60, 30–45 min, model-specific)
1. Find exact part number from fridge model label.
2. Soak new gasket in warm water 15–20 min (relaxes folds).
3. Remove old (presses into retaining groove or screws under lip).
4. Press new gasket in starting at a corner, working around. Tighten as you go.
5. Close, check gaps. Hair dryer on low can reshape stubborn spots (don't melt).
6. Let settle overnight; retest.

## Task 3 — Replace water filter

Every 6 months or ~200–300 gallons. Old filter slows flow, stops filtering, can grow bacteria.

### NSF certifications
| Standard | Removes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **[[GLOSSARY#NSF/ANSI 42 vs. 53 (water filters)|NSF 42]]** | Chlorine taste/odor, particulates | Aesthetic only |
| **[[GLOSSARY#NSF/ANSI 42 vs. 53 (water filters)|NSF 53]]** | Lead, cysts, VOCs | Health — matters for lead plumbing (pre-1986) or unknown water quality |

> ⚠️ NSF 42 only ≠ lead protection. Pre-1986 home → prioritize NSF 53.

### Replacement
1. Find filter location (upper-right fridge, base grille, or back — manual shows it).
2. Buy exact model-specific (OEM or certified aftermarket — quality varies).
3. Shut off water supply to fridge (if accessible).
4. Remove old (usually quarter-turn + pull, or release button).
5. Insert new per arrows.
6. Restore water. **Flush 2–3 gallons** to clear carbon fines. Discard first ice batch.

## Task 4 — Inspect ice maker supply line
[[GLOSSARY#Ice maker supply line|Supply line]] = hidden flood risk behind fridge.
- Pull fridge out (with helper — heavy, don't damage floor).
- Trace line wall shutoff → fridge. Plastic or braided stainless?
- **Plastic → replace with braided stainless** (~$15, 10 min — same logic as washer hoses).
- Check connections both ends for damp/crust/corrosion.
- Confirm shut-off valve works. Saddle valve (clamp-on) → consider upgrading to tee + ball valve.

## Task 5 — Interior care
- **Temp:** fridge 37–40°F, freezer 0°F (appliance thermometer confirms dial accuracy)
- Clean spills immediately (acids/sugars damage plastic)
- Don't overload (air needs to circulate)
- Cover food (releases moisture → compressor works harder)
- Vacuum gasket grooves during dollar-bill test (crumbs break seal)

## DIY / Pro boundary
| DIY | Call appliance tech |
|---|---|
| Condenser coil cleaning | Refrigerant work (EPA cert required) |
| Door gasket test + replacement | Compressor diagnosis/replacement |
| Water filter replacement | Sealed-system leaks |
| Ice maker supply line swap | Defrost system failures (heater/timer/thermostat) |
| Temperature check + interior care | Control board diagnosis |
