# Supply Lines, Hoses & Shut-off Valves

> Third note of the plumbing arc — the cheapest catastrophic-flood prevention in a home. Drain failures are slow and gradual; **supply-side** failures are pressurized bursts that flood fast. This is where the worst indoor floods come from.

## Summary
- The supply/drain split: [[Drain & Sewer Clog Prevention|drain]] failure is gradual and smelly; **supply** failure is a pressurized leak or burst — fast, flooding, expensive. Supply-side maintenance = prevent bursts (hoses) + stop water fast when one happens (valves).
- **Skill 1 — replace rubber with braided stainless.** [[GLOSSARY#Rubber supply hose|Rubber]] hoses (3–5 yr, sudden-rupture failure) → [[GLOSSARY#Braided supply line (braided stainless)|braided stainless]] (far higher burst resistance than rubber — visible fraying is the warning sign). Auto-shutoff braided hoses (~$35) sense a burst and slam closed — worth it if the washer sits over a finished space. Hand-tighten + ¼ turn; label install date; replace every 5 years.
- **Skill 2 — find and test the [[GLOSSARY#Main shut-off valve|main shut-off valve]].** When something floods, first move = stop all water to the house. Be able to do it in 30 seconds, in the dark. Tag it; tell everyone; photo it.
- Identify the valve: a [[GLOSSARY#Ball valve|ball valve]] (lever, ¼-turn, ∥ = open) is modern/reliable; a [[GLOSSARY#Gate valve|gate valve]] (round wheel, many turns) is older and **prone to seizing** — worth a pro-swap to ball eventually.
- **Exercise every valve every 6 months** ([[GLOSSARY#Exercising a valve|exercising a valve]]). Close fully, verify a faucet goes dry, reopen **slowly** to avoid [[GLOSSARY#Water hammer|water hammer]]. Repeat for every fixture valve.
- Stuck valve? Don't force — stems snap. Escalate: loosen the [[GLOSSARY#Packing nut (bonnet nut)|packing nut]] slightly → penetrating oil → gentle taps → rock back and forth. Still stuck = pro (a broken-shut valve = no water until a pro arrives).
- The two-minute flood response the above buys you: shut the main **first**, then contain/mop/photograph. Order matters — every minute = 5–10 more gallons.
- Pairs with [[Finding Leaks Early|leak detection]] (catches the slow supply drip before it bursts) and the [[GLOSSARY#Compression fitting|compression fitting]] / [[GLOSSARY#Shut-off valve (angle stop / stop valve)|fixture shut-off]] know-how behind every sink.

## Supply vs. drain (the mental model)
- **Drain failure** = backup, slow, smelly. Usually gradual.
- **Supply failure** = pressurized leak or burst. Fast, flooding, expensive. **This is where catastrophic floods come from.**

Supply-side maintenance = two things: **prevent bursts** (hoses) and **stop water fast** when one happens (valves).

## Skill 1 — Replace rubber hoses with braided stainless

### Why braided
| | Rubber | Braided stainless |
|---|---|---|
| Burst resistance | Low — fails with age | High (varies by maker; far exceeds rubber) |
| Lifespan | 3–5 yrs before risky | Longer; still inspect |
| Failure mode | Sudden rupture | Visible fraying first (usually) |
| Cost | ~$8 | ~$15–25 (auto-shutoff ~$35) |

No good reason to keep rubber hoses in service. Insurers recommend braided.

### Procedure
1. Turn off water at hot + cold shut-offs behind washer.
2. Unplug washer (or flip breaker).
3. Towel + small bucket ready (spill on disconnect).
4. Disconnect old hoses at both ends (adjustable pliers; standard ¾" thread).
5. Verify thread size against old hose.
6. Connect new braided hoses — hot to hot, cold to cold. **Hand-tighten + ¼ turn.** Don't overtighten (cuts the rubber washer).
7. Water back on slowly. Check both ends for leaks before pushing machine back.
8. **Label with install date.** Replace every 5 years.

> Also replace old rubber supply lines at sinks/toilets/dishwasher (~$5 each) as you encounter them.

> **Auto-shutoff hose (~$35):** sensor slams valve closed on burst. Worth it if washer is on 2nd floor or above a finished basement.

## Skill 2 — Find and test the main shut-off valve
When something floods, first move = stop all water to the house. Must be able to do it in the dark, in a panic, in 30 seconds.

### Find it
- Where main water line enters (basement, crawlspace, utility room, street-facing side).
- Or on the house side of the meter.
- **Tag it. Tell everyone. Photo it.**

### Identify the valve type
- **[[GLOSSARY#Ball valve|Ball valve]]** — lever handle, ¼-turn. Lever ∥ pipe = open, ⊥ = closed. Modern, reliable.
- **[[GLOSSARY#Gate valve|Gate valve]]** — round wheel, many turns. Older, cheaper, **prone to seizing**. Worth replacing with ball valve eventually (pro job).

### Test / exercise (every 6 months)
Valves that never move eventually won't move when you need them.
1. Close fully (ball: lever 90°; gate: clockwise until stop).
2. Verify — open a faucet, should go dry (brief trickle then nothing is normal).
3. **Reopen slowly** to avoid [[GLOSSARY#Water hammer|water hammer]].
   - Gate: counterclockwise until stop, then back ¼ turn.
   - Ball: lever back to ∥ — open **fully** to the stop (partial-open wears the ball).
4. Repeat for **every fixture valve** — under sinks, behind toilets, at washer.

### If a valve is stuck
Don't force — stems snap. Escalation:
1. **Loosen [[GLOSSARY#Packing nut (bonnet nut)|packing nut]]** slightly (under handle). Over-tightening is a common cause of "stuck."
2. **Penetrating oil** (PB Blaster, Kroil) around stem. Soak a few hours.
3. **Tap gently** with rubber mallet / wrench handle.
4. **Work back and forth** with pliers — ½ turn each way, gradually more. Never force.
5. **Won't move? Stop.** Seized main valve = pro replacement. A broken-shut valve = no water until pro arrives.

After freeing, retighten packing nut just enough to stop drips — should still turn smoothly.

## The two-minute flood response
The skill all the above buys you:
1. **Shut the main valve** (you know where it is, it works).
2. **Then** contain water, mop, photograph for insurance.

Order matters — water first, cleanup second. Every minute = 5–10 more gallons.

## DIY / Pro boundary
| DIY | Call a pro |
|-----|------------|
| Replace washing machine + fixture supply hoses | Replace main shut-off valve (any type) |
| Exercise valves (close/verify/reopen) | Replace seized gate valve with ball valve |
| Loosen packing nut + penetrating oil on stuck valve | Valve that won't move after the gentle steps |
| Tighten a weeping [[GLOSSARY#Compression fitting|compression fitting]] | Anything on the main supply line before the house |
