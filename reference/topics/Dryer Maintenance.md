# Dryer Maintenance

> First note of the appliance arc — and this is **fire prevention, not just upkeep.** "Failure to clean" is the #1 cause of dryer fires (~⅓ of ~2,900/yr per NFPA/USFA). Unit-specific: Maytag MED5630HC (7.3 cu.ft. front-load electric, 240V/30A).

## Summary
- Lint moves through three stages, each on its own cleaning schedule: (1) the [[GLOSSARY#Lint screen (lint filter)|lint screen]] (top of drum, every load), (2) the [[GLOSSARY#Lint trap cavity|trap cavity]] + [[GLOSSARY#Blower wheel / blower housing|blower housing]] inside the cabinet (monthly/annually), (3) the [[GLOSSARY#Dryer vent / exhaust vent|exhaust vent run]] — 4" duct to the exterior cap (annually). Skip any stage and the next one clogs faster → fire.
- **Tier 1 (homeowner, no panels removed):** clean the screen every load (monthly: scrub mesh with soapy water — fabric-softener film chokes airflow even when it looks clean); vacuum the trap cavity monthly; wipe the [[GLOSSARY#Moisture sensor strips|moisture sensor strips]] with rubbing alcohol (softener film → false "dry" → damp loads).
- A lit [[GLOSSARY#Check Vent light|Check Vent]] indicator = restricted exhaust airflow. Clean the screen → then the full vent run → then a Tier 2 deep clean. A persistent light you ignore is a fire precursor, not a nuisance.
- **Annual exhaust vent clean:** unplug + flip the 240V double-pole breaker, pull the dryer, vacuum the exhaust stub + first feet of duct, clear the exterior cap. If the duct is vinyl or foil spiral-wire, **replace with rigid/semi-rigid aluminum** (~$10) — vinyl/foil sags, traps lint, and is the most fire-prone option.
- **Tier 2 (annual interior deep clean):** remove the rear panel (5/16" hex-head screws — photo the layout first, not all same length) and vacuum the blower housing, motor, cabinet base, and around the [[GLOSSARY#Heating element|heating element]]. This is the lint that ignites. Don't touch the element (fragile, runs red-hot); don't dislodge wiring connectors.
- Power-off is two-step and non-negotiable: unplug **and** flip the 240V double-pole breaker; verify dead with a non-contact voltage tester.
- Verify with an empty high-heat load: strong warm airflow at the exterior cap = good; weak/none = restriction — don't use the dryer until cleared.
- Failure mode here is **fire**, not just odor — contrast with the [[Washer Maintenance|washer]] where the failure mode is mildew. Pairs with the [[Supply Lines, Hoses & Shut-off Valves|supply hose]] check behind the washer (a burst there floods the same laundry room).

## Full procedure

> Hands-on tasks live in [[ACTION-ITEMS]].

> ⚠️ **This is fire prevention, not just upkeep.** "Failure to clean" is the #1 cause of dryer fires (~⅓ of ~2,900/yr per NFPA/USFA). The screen catches the visible bulk; what gets *past* it, into the vent run and cabinet, is the fire hazard.

## Where lint goes (3 stages)
1. **[[GLOSSARY#Lint screen (lint filter)|Lint screen]]** — top of drum, catches the bulk
2. **[[GLOSSARY#Lint trap cavity|Trap cavity]] + [[GLOSSARY#Blower wheel / blower housing|blower housing]]** — inside cabinet, accumulates over years
3. **[[GLOSSARY#Dryer vent / exhaust vent|Exhaust vent run]]** — 4" duct through wall to exterior cap

Every stage needs cleaning on a different schedule. Skip any stage → next one clogs faster → fire.

## Tier 1 — Homeowner scope (no panels removed)

### 1. Lint screen — every load
Pull, roll lint off with fingers, trash it (not down a sink — clogs drains). **Monthly deep clean:** scrub mesh with nylon brush + warm soapy water; dry fully. Fabric-softener film reduces airflow even when it "looks" clean.

### 2. Vacuum lint trap cavity — monthly
Screen out, crevice tool down into the slot as far as it reaches.

### 3. Wipe moisture sensor strips — monthly
Two metal strips near the lint screen opening inside the drum. Rubbing alcohol on a cloth. Fabric-softener film → false "dry" reading → damp loads. 10-second fix.

### 4. Check Vent light response
[[GLOSSARY#Check Vent light|Check Vent indicator]] on = exhaust airflow restricted.
1. Clean lint screen.
2. Still on → full vent run clean (step 5).
3. Still on → restriction inside cabinet → Tier 2 deep clean or pro.

**Persistent light you ignore = precursor to fire.** Action item, not nuisance.

### 5. Clean exhaust vent run — annually
1. Unplug dryer + flip 240V double-pole breaker at panel.
2. Pull dryer out, access back.
3. Disconnect vent clamp, pull duct off dryer exhaust stub.
4. Vacuum dryer exhaust stub + first few feet of duct.
5. Go to exterior cap. Pop it open, vacuum/clear lint, verify flap swings free.
6. Long/unreachable run → dryer-vent cleaning brush kit (~$20–30) fed from either end, or hire a vent-cleaning service.
7. Reconnect with clamp, push back, restore power.

> ⚠️ **Duct material:** if vinyl or foil spiral-wire → replace with **rigid or semi-rigid aluminum** (~$10). Vinyl/foil sags, traps lint, isn't code-compliant for dryer exhaust. Most fire-prone option. IRC M1502: the **transition duct** (dryer to wall) must be listed, ≤8 ft, 4" diameter; the **run** (wall to exterior cap) should be **rigid metal**, max **35 ft equivalent length** (subtract ~5 ft per 90° elbow). A run longer than 35 ft can't be cleaned into compliance and needs rerouting — a long run that won't stay clear is a fire hazard regardless of cleaning cadence.

**Annual exterior check:** dryer running, go to the cap. Strong warm airflow = good. Weak = restriction. None = blockage — don't use dryer until cleared.

## Tier 2 — Interior deep clean (annual)
> ⚠️ **The seam.** Involves removing cabinet panels + working near the heating element and blower. DIY-scope with basic tools, but more involved. **Not comfortable? Stop and hire a pro vent-cleaning service — many include interior cleaning.**

Even with perfect screen discipline, lint gets past it over years and coats the blower housing, element shroud, and internal ductwork. That's what ignites.

### Tools
- **Nut driver set (¼" + 5/16")** — Maytag cabinet screws are these hex sizes
- Shop vac (crevice + brush attachments) — the main tool
- Work light / headlamp
- Long flexible brush (vent brush or bottle brush)
- Mask + safety glasses (lint is fine + airborne once disturbed)
- Optional work gloves (sheet-metal edges sharp)

### Procedure
1. **Power off definitively:** unplug + flip 240V double-pole breaker. Verify dead with non-contact voltage tester.
2. Pull dryer out, disconnect vent duct + power cord from wall.
3. **Open rear panel** — 5/16" hex-head screws around the perimeter, lift off. **Photo the screw layout first** (not all same length).
4. Locate blower housing (lower-left/lower-center, round, connected to exhaust duct) — bulk of internal lint.
5. **Vacuum everything visible** — blower housing, motor, cabinet base, around heating element housing. Brush dislodges stuck lint, vacuum removes it. **Don't touch the element itself** (fragile).
6. Clean internal duct path blower → exhaust stub. Vent brush loosens what vacuum can't reach.
7. Vacuum back of lint trap cavity from inside the cabinet.
8. Inspect drum seal + belt while open (note fraying/glazing — separate repair). Vacuum felt seals.
9. Reassemble reverse. Correct screws per your photo. Hand-tight (sheet-metal).
10. Reconnect vent + power. Restore breaker. Empty load, high heat, 10 min — verify strong airflow at exterior cap.

> ⚠️ **Two cautions inside cabinet:** (1) Don't touch heating element — fragile wire coil, runs red-hot; vacuum *around* it. (2) Don't dislodge wiring connectors — a misconnected thermistor or thermal fuse stops the dryer or defeats overheat protection (new fire risk). Note any that come off.

## Cadence — your unit
| Frequency | Task |
|---|---|
| Every load | Clean lint screen |
| Monthly | Vacuum trap cavity; wipe moisture sensors (rubbing alcohol); deep-clean screen mesh |
| Annually | Clean vent run to exterior; verify exterior airflow; interior deep clean (or pro); verify duct material |
| Trigger | Check Vent light → vent clean → deep clean. Damp loads → clean sensors. |
