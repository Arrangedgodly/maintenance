# Smoke & CO Detectors

> The cheapest life insurance in a home. Covers the two distinct threats ([[GLOSSARY#Ionization smoke alarm|smoke]] vs. [[GLOSSARY#Carbon monoxide (CO)|CO]]), where each detector type belongs, and the cadence that keeps them alive.

## Summary
Two threats need two different alarms: [[GLOSSARY#Ionization smoke alarm|ionization]] (fast-flaming) vs. [[GLOSSARY#Photoelectric smoke alarm|photoelectric]] (smoldering) — NFPA says use both, ideally as [[GLOSSARY#Dual-sensor smoke alarm|dual-sensor]] units. [[GLOSSARY#Carbon monoxide (CO)|CO]] is odorless gas from any [[GLOSSARY#Gas valve / control valve (gas only)|gas]] appliance (furnace, [[Water Heater Maintenance|water heater]], fireplace, stove, attached garage) — detected only by a [[GLOSSARY#CO alarm|CO alarm]] with a ~7-yr life vs. smoke's 10-yr. Placement is non-negotiable: smoke in every bedroom + outside sleeping areas + every level (NFPA 72); CO near sleeping areas and 5–20 ft from fuel-burning gear. The highest-ROI upgrade for an existing home is [[GLOSSARY#Interconnection (smoke/CO alarms)|wireless-interconnected]] [[GLOSSARY#10-year sealed battery (smoke/CO)|10-year sealed]] alarms — no electrician, basement fire wakes everyone. Avoid the ionization-in-the-kitchen trap (false alarms → pulled battery → no protection); use photoelectric or a [[GLOSSARY#Heat detector|heat detector]] ≥10 ft from the stove. Two replacement clocks to track: smoke = 10 yr from manufacture date, CO = ~7 yr, [[GLOSSARY#Combination smoke + CO alarm|combination units]] = shorter of the two. Test monthly, swap batteries at [[Master Seasonal Checklist|daylight-saving]], never re-enter after a CO alarm.

## Two threats, two alarms
| | Smoke alarm | [[GLOSSARY#CO alarm|CO alarm]] |
|---|---|---|
| Detects | Smoke particles | [[GLOSSARY#Carbon monoxide (CO)|Carbon monoxide]] gas |
| Threat | Visible fire | Invisible, odorless gas |
| Sources | Any fire | Gas furnace, gas water heater, fireplace, gas stove, attached garage |
| Response | Get out, call 911 | Get out, call 911/gas utility. **Do not re-enter until cleared.** |

> ⚠️ **CO re-entry is a documented casualty pattern.** CO incapacitates quickly. People who go back in to "air it out" or save pets often don't come out. Stay out until fire dept/gas utility clears it.

## Part 1 — Smoke alarm types

| | [[GLOSSARY#Ionization smoke alarm|Ionization]] | [[GLOSSARY#Photoelectric smoke alarm|Photoelectric]] |
|---|---|---|
| Best at | Fast-flaming (grease, paper) | Smoldering (cigarettes, wiring) |
| Kitchen | Nuisance trips (battery gets pulled) | Fewer false alarms |
| Place in | Living areas away from kitchen | Bedrooms, hallways, near kitchen |

**NFPA: use both technologies.** Either dual-sensor units ([[GLOSSARY#Dual-sensor smoke alarm|both in one]]) or a mix.

> ⚠️ **Ionization-in-the-kitchen trap:** false alarm → homeowner pulls battery → no protection. Use photoelectric or [[GLOSSARY#Heat detector|heat detector]] ≥10 ft from stove.

## Part 2 — Placement

### Smoke alarms (NFPA 72)
- **Inside every bedroom** (non-negotiable)
- **Outside each sleeping area** (hallway serving bedrooms)
- **Every level** including basement + habitable attic
- **Basement:** ceiling at bottom of stairs leading up
- **Ceiling mount preferred** (smoke rises). Wall: high, 4–12" from ceiling

### Smoke alarm pitfalls
| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Within 10 ft of cooking | Nuisance alarms |
| Within ~10 ft of a bathroom with tub/shower | Steam false-alarms (Kidde/First Alert) |
| In the garage | Exhaust/dust — use heat detector |
| Within 4" of wall-ceiling corner | Dead-air space (older guidance; the 4-inch rule was removed from NFPA 72 in 2016 — current hard rule is within 36" of a paddle-fan blade tip and >36" from supply/return registers) |
| Near windows/doors/fans/registers | Air currents push smoke away |
| In vaulted ceiling peak/nook | Dead-air trap — 3 ft from peak |

### CO alarms
- **Every level** + **near sleeping areas**
- **5–20 ft from gas furnace, water heater, fireplace** (close for early warning, far enough to avoid startup puffs)
- **≥15 ft from gas stove** (normal-use CO)
- **Near (not in) attached garage**
- Wall mount ~5 ft high (breathing level) typical; ceiling OK. CO density ≈ air, diffuses evenly — height matters less than for smoke.

## Part 3 — Interconnection
When one triggers, **all sound.** Critical for sleeping occupants far from the source alarm.

| | Hardwired (AC) | Wireless (RF) |
|---|---|---|
| Reliability | Highest (NFPA 94% operated) | Slightly lower, effective |
| Retrofit | Electrician + wall-fishing + permit | DIY — swap units, pair automatically |
| Best for | New construction | Existing homes |

> **Highest-ROI safety upgrade for existing home:** wireless-interconnected [[GLOSSARY#10-year sealed battery (smoke/CO)|10-year sealed]] alarms. No electrician, no battery changes for a decade, basement fire wakes everyone upstairs.

## Part 4 — Replacement (two clocks)

| Detector | Replace at |
|---|---|
| Smoke alarm | **10 years** from manufacture date (stamped on back) |
| CO alarm | **7–10 years** (varies by manufacturer — check the date stamped on the unit, UL 2034 sets the replacement date per-maker) |
| Combination | **Shorter of the two** — check the stamp |

> ⚠️ **End-of-life chirp ≠ low battery.** CO alarms chirp at end-of-life almost identically to low-battery. New battery doesn't fix it. Check the date — 7+ yr = replace unit.

**Track it:** write install date + replacement year on each unit with Sharpie. Check at daylight-saving changes ([[Master Seasonal Checklist|seasonal checklist]]).

## Part 5 — Testing + maintenance
- **Monthly:** press TEST on every alarm.
- **Twice yearly (daylight-saving):** swap batteries (replaceable type). 10-yr sealed: just test.
- **Twice yearly:** vacuum/blow out dust (dust → false alarms + delayed real ones).
- **Never paint an alarm** (seals sensing chamber).
- **Repeated false trips, not near kitchen/bath:** dusty or end-of-life → clean, replace if persists.

### Chirping decoder
| Sound | Means |
|---|---|
| Single chirp every 30–60s | Low battery (replaceable) or end-of-life (sealed/CO) — check date |
| Continuous loud alarm | Active event — evacuate |
| Voice: "Carbon monoxide!" | CO warning on combo voice unit — evacuate |
| 3 chirps, pause, repeat | Many models: malfunction / end-of-life — replace |

## Part 6 — Your home's specific needs
Equipment in NOTES: Carrier 58TP0 gas furnace + Bradford White RG250T6N gas water heater + (presumably) attached garage → **CO coverage as critical as smoke.**

- **Bedrooms + hallway:** dual-sensor or photoelectric smoke (life-safety for sleeping occupants)
- **Living areas:** dual-sensor smoke
- **Kitchen:** photoelectric ≥10 ft from stove, or heat detector
- **Basement near furnace + water heater:** **combination smoke + CO alarm** within 5–20 ft (your CO early-warning location)
- **Each level near sleeping areas:** CO alarm
- **All interconnected** — wireless 10-yr sealed if not hardwired
