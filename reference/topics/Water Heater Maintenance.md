# Water Heater Maintenance

> Final note of the plumbing arc — the three annual tasks (flush, T&P test, anode rod) that take an 8-year tank to 15+. Unit-specific: Bradford White RG250T6N (50-gal natural gas).

## Summary
- Know your type first: **gas** (flue out top, gas line, burner, OFF/PILOT/ON valve), **electric** (no vent/gas, double-pole breaker, two element access panels), or **tankless** (separate coverage).
- **Three annual tasks, done together:** (1) flush the tank to remove [[GLOSSARY#Sediment|sediment]], (2) test the [[GLOSSARY#T&P valve (temperature & pressure relief valve)|T&P valve]], (3) inspect the [[GLOSSARY#Anode rod (sacrificial anode)|anode rod]] — the single biggest factor in tank lifespan.
- **Shutdown is type-specific and the dangerous step.** Gas → valve to PILOT/VACATION, let water cool. Electric → breaker OFF at the panel — **non-negotiable**: a powered [[GLOSSARY#Heating element (electric only)|heating element]] in an empty tank burns out in seconds (the #1 DIY mistake).
- Flush: attach a hose to the [[GLOSSARY#Drain valve|drain valve]], drain, then pulse cold water to stir sediment until it runs clear. Refill fully (hot faucet running steady, no air) **before** restoring power/gas.
- T&P test: bucket under the discharge tube, lift lever briefly — steady hot flow, snaps back, flow stops. Keeps dripping = flush deposit; won't open = seized, replace immediately (a seized T&P is a hidden bomb).
- The [[GLOSSARY#Anode rod (sacrificial anode)|anode rod]] corrodes *instead of* the tank. >50% gone or bare wire = replace. Magnesium (soft water), aluminum (hard water), zinc-aluminum (sulfur smell), or powered/flexible if ceiling clearance is tight.
- **Your Bradford White RG250T6N quirk:** the anode rod is built into the **hot-water outlet nipple** — no separate top hex. Access it by disconnecting the hot supply line and unscrewing the outlet nipple. OEM rod: 415-49560-08, rigid 39-1/8" (measure clearance first — under ~4 ft = flexible/powered rod).
- Gas-only annual visual checks: [[GLOSSARY#Flue / vent (gas only)|flue]] intact (disconnected = CO in the house), steady blue burner flame, clean flame-arrestor screen at base, no rust/water at the tank base.
- Set temperature to **120°F** — below scalding, slows sediment, saves energy.
- Pro territory: any gas-valve/burner/pilot work beyond the dial, flue repair, tank-base leak (tank failure), recurring T&P drips (pressure problem). Ties to [[Finding Leaks Early|leak detection]] (rust at the base = before-it-floods warning).

## Full procedure

> Hands-on tasks live in [[ACTION-ITEMS]].

## Know your type first
- **Gas:** flue/vent pipe out top, gas line with shutoff, burner at bottom (visible through sight glass), gas control valve with OFF/PILOT/ON.
- **Electric:** no vent, no gas. Wires to a double-pole breaker. Two element access panels on the side.
- **Tankless:** different animal — flag for separate coverage.

## The three annual tasks
1. **Flush the tank** — removes [[GLOSSARY#Sediment|sediment]].
2. **Test the T&P valve** — confirms safety valve works.
3. **Inspect the [[GLOSSARY#Anode rod (sacrificial anode)|anode rod]]** — the part that determines tank lifespan.

Do all three together, once a year.

## Shutdown — different for gas vs. electric
> ⚠️ **Gas:** set gas valve to **PILOT** (or VACATION). Don't turn fully OFF unless you know how to relight the pilot. Let water cool a few hours.
> ⚠️ **Electric:** flip breaker **OFF** at the panel. Non-negotiable — a powered element in an empty tank **burns out in seconds** (the #1 DIY mistake).

## Flush procedure
1. Shut off cold water supply valve on top of tank.
2. Attach garden hose to [[GLOSSARY#Drain valve|drain valve]] at base. Run to drain/sink/outdoors.
3. Open a hot faucet (tub ideal) — lets air in so tank drains.
4. Open drain valve. Let tank drain fully (hot + murky at first).
5. **Flush:** hose still on + drain valve open → open cold supply on top 30–60s to stir sediment. Repeat until water runs clear.
6. Close drain valve. Remove hose.
7. **Refill:** open cold supply on top. Hot faucet open — air sputters out. When it runs steady with no air, tank is full.
8. **Restore power/gas** — only after tank is full (hot faucet running steady, no air).

### If drain valve is clogged (sediment)
- Needle-nose pliers / stiff wire to clear opening (hose attached, water off, valve open).
- Momentary cold-water backflush (briefly open cold supply with drain valve open).
- Won't clear + old tank → drain valve replacement (pro if uncomfortable; standard threaded fitting but tank must be empty first).

## T&P valve test
[[GLOSSARY#T&P valve (temperature & pressure relief valve)|T&P valve]] = safety release. Seized shut = hidden bomb.
1. Bucket under discharge tube.
2. Lift lever briefly — water flows steadily (hot — hands clear).
3. Release — snaps back, flow stops immediately.
- **Keeps dripping:** lift/reseat a few times to flush deposit. Still drips → valve fouled → replace.
- **Won't open:** valve seized → replace immediately. Don't leave a tank in service with a non-functional T&P.

## Anode rod inspection
The rod corrodes *instead of* the tank. When consumed, tank starts rusting. Replacing it extends tank life.

### Inspect
1. Shut off power/gas + cold supply (no full drain needed).
2. Find hex head — usually on top, under a plastic cap. Sometimes combined with hot outlet nipple.
3. Open a hot faucet to relieve pressure.
4. Loosen hex head with socket (usually 1-1/16" or 1½") + breaker bar. **Have a helper brace the tank** — don't let wrench torque twist it.
5. Pull rod out. Inspect.

| Condition | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Thick, intact metal | Reinstall; check next year |
| Pitted, >50% gone, bare wire stretches | Replace |
| Just a thin wire core | Replace **now** — tank unprotected |

### Replacement rod types
- **Magnesium** — best protection, soft water, ~1–3 yr
- **Aluminum** — hard water, ~3–5 yr
- **Zinc-aluminum** — sulfur/rotten-egg smell
- **Powered (electronic)** — doesn't consume; good if tired of replacing rods
- **Flexible/segmented** — if ceiling clearance < ~3 ft (measure before loosening)

## Gas-only: annual visual checks (observation, not work)
- **[[GLOSSARY#Flue / vent (gas only)|Flue pipe]]** — connected and intact all the way up? No corrosion gaps? (Disconnected flue = CO in the house.)
- **Burner flame** (sight glass) — steady blue (maybe slight orange tips). Mostly yellow = incomplete combustion → pro.
- **Gas smell** — if ever smelled near heater, leave + call gas utility. Don't DIY gas.
- **Tank base** — rust streaks / water below burner = tank failing from within → plan replacement.

## Temperature setting
**120°F recommended.** Below scalding risk, slows sediment, saves energy. Higher = scald danger + faster sediment. Dial it down if above 120.

### Pulling your anode rod (outlet-nipple method — not a top hex)
1. Shut down for gas (valve to PILOT/VACATION, cold supply off, hot faucet open to relieve pressure). Drain a few gallons so water isn't at the very top.
2. Disconnect the hot supply line at the top of the tank.
3. Unscrew the outlet nipple/anode assembly with a pipe wrench or 1½" socket + breaker bar. **Brace the tank** against torque.
4. Lift the rod straight out — needs ~full tank height + 12–18" clearance above.
5. Inspect (intact → reinstall; >50% gone → replace; bare wire → replace now).
6. Reinstall with thread seal tape/pipe dope on nipple threads. Reconnect hot supply. Refill + bleed before restoring gas.

### Clearance check (do this first)
Rigid OEM rod = 39-1/8". Measure top-of-tank to ceiling. **Under ~4 ft of clearance** → can't withdraw the rigid rod. Options:
- **Flexible / segmented aluminum anode rod** (bends to exit; confirm ¾" NPT matches nipple).
- **Powered (electronic) anode rod** (doesn't consume — only fight the clearance once).

### Gas valve & ignition
- **Piezo push-button igniter** (red/black button on gas control). No standing pilot.
- Relight: gas valve to PILOT, hold in, push piezo repeatedly until pilot catches, hold 30–60s, release.
- **Read the lighting-instructions sticker on the tank first.** Defender Safety System has specific steps.
- **Pilot won't stay lit?** Most common = failed thermocouple OR blocked flame-arrestor screen at base (trips the spillage sensor). Both repairable; gas-train diagnosis = reasonable pro call. Don't force it.

### Annual task list (this specific unit)
- [ ] Flush tank (standard gas procedure; atmospheric vent flue through center is normal)
- [ ] Test T&P valve (standard; side-mounted with downward discharge tube)
- [ ] Pull + inspect anode rod (**outlet-nipple method**, not a top hex)
- [ ] Visual: flue/vent intact to chimney; gas connection dry/smell-free; burner flame steady blue (sight glass); **flame arrestor screen at base clean** (vacuum gently — blocked screen trips spillage sensor → shutdown)
- [ ] Drain pan (if present) — confirm drain line clear

> **Warranty:** 6-yr tank. Serial BC54925100 — Bradford White encodes the manufacture date as the **first two letters**: first letter = year, second letter = month (per [BW's date-code chart](https://www.bradfordwhite.com/bw-faq/how-to-read-the-serial-number-date-code-reference-chart/)). | DIY | Call a pro |
|-----|------------|
