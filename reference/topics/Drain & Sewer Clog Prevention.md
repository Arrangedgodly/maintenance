# Drain & Sewer Clog Prevention

> First note of the plumbing arc — the mental model for drains (fixture → main line → [[GLOSSARY#Sewer lateral|sewer lateral]]) and the prevention routine that stops repeat clogs. Grew directly out of a real basement sewer-line backup cleared with an [[GLOSSARY#Auger / plumber's snake|auger]] and a [[GLOSSARY#Drain bladder|drain bladder]].

## Summary
- Three levels of drain: (1) the [[GLOSSARY#Fixture drain|fixture drain]] (one sink/tub/toilet), (2) the [[GLOSSARY#Main line (main drain)|main line]] (all fixtures combine), (3) the [[GLOSSARY#Sewer lateral|sewer lateral]] (house → city main).
- **Key diagnostic rule:** a level-2/3 clog backs up at the **lowest drain in the house** (often the basement floor drain). A single-fixture clog stays isolated.
- Level 1 prevention = behavior: never put [[GLOSSARY#FOG (fats, oils, grease)|grease]], coffee grounds, eggshells, or pasta/rice down the kitchen sink; hair screens on tubs; **only** human waste + toilet paper down the toilet — "flushable" wipes are the #1 preventable sewer clog nationwide.
- Skip chemical drain cleaners for prevention — lye/acid damage old pipes and endanger whoever opens the [[GLOSSARY#P-trap|trap]] or [[GLOSSARY#Auger / plumber's snake|augers]] the line next.
- Level 2/3 main-line clogs are usually [[GLOSSARY#Root intrusion|tree roots]] (esp. in old clay/cast-iron), snagged wipes, grease buildup, or collapsed/bellied pipe. Recurring clogs + gurgling toilets + multiple slow drains = get a **camera inspection**, not another round of augering.
- After a clearing: behavior change + annual [[GLOSSARY#Foaming root killer|foaming root killer]] (down a toilet, after mechanical clearing — preventive, not a substitute). Aggressive recurring roots = the real fix is pipe repair (trenchless lining or PVC replacement), pro-only.
- Know your [[GLOSSARY#Cleanout|cleanout]] now — the 4" capped access to the main line, usually outside within 5–10 ft of the foundation. Photograph it; don't hunt during a flood.
- **You own the [[GLOSSARY#Sewer lateral|sewer lateral]]** all the way to the city-main connection (often under the street, not the property line). Check "[city] sewer lateral responsibility" and ask about service-line coverage (~$30–60/yr) before a $5k+ bill.
- Leads into [[Finding Leaks Early|leak detection]] and [[Supply Lines, Hoses & Shut-off Valves|supply-side flood prevention]] — the supply/drain split that frames the whole plumbing arc.

## The mental model: three levels of drain
Clogs at different levels need different responses.

1. **[[GLOSSARY#Fixture drain|Fixture drain]]** — one sink/tub/toilet/washing machine. Clog affects one fixture.
2. **[[GLOSSARY#Main line (main drain)|Main line / main drain]]** — all fixtures combine. Clog affects the whole house.
3. **[[GLOSSARY#Sewer lateral|Sewer lateral]]** — underground pipe from house to city main.

**Key diagnostic rule:** a clog at level 2 or 3 backs up at the **lowest drain in the house** (often the basement floor drain). A single-fixture clog doesn't cross-contaminate.

## Level 1 — Fixture drains (prevention)

### Kitchen sink — never put down
- **Grease / fat / oil** — #1 cause. Liquid hot → solidifies in cold pipe. Jar it, trash it.
- **Coffee grounds** — settle & pack like sediment.
- **Eggshells** — membrane catches; shell grinds to grit.
- **Pasta, rice, flour** — expand and turn to paste.

**Prevention:** sink strainer ($3). Run hot water 20–30s after disposal use. Monthly kettle of hot (not boiling — protects PVC) water to melt minor grease film.

### Bathroom sink & tub
Culprits: **hair + soap scum.**
- **Hair screen / shower drain cover** ($2–5), cleaned weekly.
- For a slow drain: remove the pop-up stopper, pull the hair clump, flush hot water. Usually a 2-min fix.

### Toilet — only two things ever
**Human waste and toilet paper. Nothing else.**
- **"Flushable" wipes** — #1 preventable sewer clog nationwide. Do **not** break down. Never flush, regardless of label.
- No paper towels, tissues, feminine products, cotton balls/swabs, dental floss, kitty litter, condoms.

### Monthly maintenance (not a clog-clearer)
Hot (not boiling) water down each drain. Optional: ½ cup baking soda + 1 cup vinegar, cover 15 min, flush hot water. Dissolves minor buildup before it becomes a clog.

> ⚠️ **Skip chemical drain cleaners** (Drano, Liquid-Plumr) for prevention. Lye/acid can damage old pipes and create a hazard for whoever later opens the trap or augers the line. Reserve for emergencies; never use before calling a pro.

## Level 2/3 — Main line & sewer lateral

### What clogs a main line
- **[[GLOSSARY#Root intrusion|Tree roots]]** — leading cause of recurring clogs, esp. older **clay or cast-iron** pipe. Enter through joints/cracks, then grow.
- **Flushed debris** (wipes) that snagged at a joint or root mass.
- **Grease buildup** in the long horizontal run.
- **Collapsed / bellied pipe** — physical obstruction (crack, sag, separation). Flushing won't fix it.
- **Scale** in old iron pipe.

### Is it root intrusion? (signs)
- Multiple slow drains throughout the house
- Gurgling toilets when other drains run
- Sewage smell / backups at the lowest drain
- Recurring clogs (cleared, back in 2–6 months)
- Older home (pre-1980s → likely clay/cast-iron lateral)

If 2+ apply → the next step isn't more augering, it's a **camera inspection** by a plumber. Most valuable diagnostic for recurring clogs.

### Prevention after a clearing
1. **Behavior change** — never-flush rules above. Alone resolves many recurring clogs.
2. **Annual [[GLOSSARY#Foaming root killer|foaming root killer]]** — copper sulfate or dichlobenil, down a toilet, once/year, after a clearing. Kills root tips before they grow. Preventive, not a substitute for clearing.
3. **Camera inspection every 1–2 years** if recurring roots.

> ⚠️ **Aggressive recurring roots:** the real fix is pipe repair — trenchless lining (CIPP) or PVC replacement of the damaged section. Pro-only, not cheap, but the actual cure. Repeatedly augering every few months is symptom treatment, not disease treatment.

## Know your [[GLOSSARY#Cleanout|cleanout]]
The 4" capped pipe giving direct access to the main line — the entry point for augering, bladder-flushing, camera inspection. **Locate it now, before the next emergency.**

- **Outside, within 5–10 ft of foundation**, between house and street. White/black PVC or cast-iron, threaded cap, often in a ground-level box. May be hidden by landscaping.
- **Indoor, in basement/utility area**, on the main line just before it exits the foundation.
- Older homes: may be buried or absent — having one installed makes every future clearing cheaper.

Photo the location and cap type. Don't hunt for it during a flood.

## Who owns the problem: you or the city?
- **Homeowner owns the [[GLOSSARY#Sewer lateral|sewer lateral]]** — from house, under property, to the connection with the city main. Often **under the street**, not at the property line.
- **City owns the main** and handles clogs there.

**Do two things:**
1. Search "[your city] sewer lateral responsibility" — boundary varies by municipality.
2. Ask utility/insurer about **service line coverage** — a cheap rider (~$30–60/yr) covering lateral repair. Most learn it exists only after a $5k+ bill.

**Call the city first** if: multiple neighbors backing up simultaneously, or backup happens with no water use in your home → clog may be in the city main.

## DIY / Pro boundary
| DIY | Call a pro |
|-----|------------|
| Sink/tub/toilet hand auger | Camera inspection of main line |
| Hair removal from pop-up stopper | **Hydro jetting** (pro equipment; can damage old pipes) |
| P-trap removal & cleaning | Recurring main-line clogs (needs diagnosis) |
| Drain bladder flush | Any clog with sewage backup |
| Monthly hot-water/enzyme maintenance | Suspected collapsed/bellied pipe |
