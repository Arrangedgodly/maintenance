# Homeowner Starter Toolkit

> What to own, what to rent, what to borrow — consolidated from the tools scattered across every other note in the vault. The one buying decision that matters more than any specific brand is your [[GLOSSARY#Cordless drill/driver|cordless battery platform]].

## Summary
The **3-use rule**: use a tool more than 3×/yr → buy; one-off project → rent; occasional + neighbor has one → borrow. The **core hand-tool kit** (hammer, screwdrivers, tape, pliers ×2, adjustable wrench, utility knife, level, hex keys, putty knife, flashlight, stud finder) covers ~80% of household repairs for ~$200. Add a [[GLOSSARY#Cordless drill/driver|cordless drill]] as your first power tool, bought as a kit with battery + charger — and **commit to one battery platform** (Ryobi ONE+ for value + range, DeWalt/Makita for pro quality) so every future cordless bare-tool purchase shares batteries. Maintenance-specific tools ([[GLOSSARY#Non-contact voltage tester|non-contact voltage tester]], basin wrench, [[GLOSSARY#Fin comb (fin straightener)|fin comb]], taping knives, [[GLOSSARY#Shop vac (wet/dry vacuum)|shop vac]]) are bought when you do the corresponding task — don't buy everything upfront. Rent pressure washers, tile saws, drywall lifts; borrow miter saws and nail guns. **Avoid pre-built "150-piece kits"** — you'll use 10 of the 150 pieces, and those 10 are lower quality than buying individually.

## The own / rent / borrow decision

| Question | If YES → | If NO → |
|---|---|---|
| Will I use this more than 3–4 times a year? | **Buy** | Consider rent/borrow |
| Is it under $50–100? | **Buy** | Consider rent/borrow |
| Is it for a one-time project? | **Rent or Borrow** | — |
| Is it expensive and specialized? | **Rent** | — |

## Tier 2 — The power tool platform

**The single most important buying decision:** pick ONE battery platform and stay on it. The battery + charger is the expensive part; bare tools are cheap once you own those.

| Platform | Best for | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Ryobi (18V ONE+)** | Best value; ~300+ tools on one battery; homeowner-adequate quality | Huge — drills to trimmers to fans |
| **DeWalt (20V MAX)** | Pro-sumer; durable; widely available | Large; strong in power tools |
| **Makita (18V LXT)** | Pro-sumer; excellent; trades-favored | Large; premium pricing |
| **Milwaukee (M18)** | Professional-grade; most expensive | Large; overkill for most homeowners |

> For maintenance (not construction), **Ryobi ONE+** is usually right: quality is adequate, range means every future cordless shares one battery, price ~half of DeWalt/Makita. Heavier and less refined — a fine trade for homeowner use.

## Tier 3 — Maintenance-specific tools (buy when you do the task)

### Electrical
- Non-contact voltage tester (~$15)
- Digital multimeter (~$25–40)
- Circuit-breaker finder (~$25)
- Wire strippers / combo tool (~$15)
- Insulated screwdrivers (~$15)

### Plumbing
- Basin wrench (~$15) — faucet nuts behind sinks
- Strap wrench (~$10) — chrome escutcheons
- Cartridge puller (brand-specific, ~$10)

### HVAC
- 5/16" nut driver (~$8) — Carrier cabinet screws
- Fin comb, 25 FPI (~$10) — Carrier 24SPA
- Wet/dry shop vac (~$50) — condensate, drains, coils

### Drywall + general
- Taping knives: 4", 8", 12" (~$20 set)
- Sanding sponge (~$4)
- Caulk gun (~$8)

- Safety glasses + work gloves (~$15)

## What to borrow

- **Miter saw** — trim work; borrow until you know you need one
- **Nail gun** — framing/trim; occasional
- **Table saw** — sheet goods; one project
- **Chain saw** — tree work; a few times a decade
- **Rototiller / lawn aerator** — annual or less
- Check: neighbors, family, **tool libraries** (many cities have them), buy-nothing groups

## Safety gear (always own)

| Item | When |
|---|---|
| Safety glasses | Any drilling, cutting, snipping, under-sink work |
| Work gloves (nitrile + leather) | Plumbing, sheet metal, disposer, drywall |
| Dust mask / N95 | Sanding drywall, insulation, disposer cleaning |
| Headlamp | Under sinks, attic, power outages |
| Knee pads | Under-sink plumbing, flooring, baseboard |

## The pre-built kit warning

Avoid "$99 / 150-piece homeowner kits" in plastic cases — quantity over quality. You'll use 10 of the 150 pieces, and those 10 are lower quality than buying individually. The specific tools you need (fin comb, basin wrench, voltage tester, cartridge puller) are never in a kit. Buy individually as the work demands.
