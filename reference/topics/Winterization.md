## The water-freeze mental model
Water expands ~9% when it freezes — strong enough to split copper, crack PVC, tear fittings. Every winterization task uses one of three defenses:
1. **Remove the water** (drain it) → hose bibbs, sprinklers
2. **Keep it warm** (insulate / heat) → exposed pipes, heat cable
3. **Let it expand** (relieve pressure) → drip a faucet

## Part 1 — Outdoor faucets (#1 freeze-failure)
Most common freeze failure: split pipe in exterior wall/crawlspace feeding outdoor faucet. Splits, thaws, floods — often while away/asleep. 5 min/faucet to prevent.

### Know which type
| Type | ID | Winterization |
|---|---|---|
| [[GLOSSARY#Frost-free sillcock (freeze-proof faucet)|Frost-free sillcock]] | Handle extends 6–12" into heated wall (post-1990 homes usually) | **Disconnect the hose.** That's it — self-drains. Hose left attached = traps water = defeats design. |
| Standard hose bibb | Valve right at handle, outside heated wall (older homes) | Close interior shutoff, open exterior faucet, leave open. |

### Standard bibb procedure
1. Locate [[GLOSSARY#Hose bibb shutoff (interior)|interior shutoff]] on pipe feeding faucet (basement/crawlspace/utility).
2. Close interior valve.
3. Disconnect hose.
4. Open outdoor faucet fully → [[GLOSSARY#Drain-back (winterization)|drain-back]].
5. **Leave outdoor faucet OPEN for winter** (closed traps residual water → burst).
6. If interior valve has drain cap → open to drain valve body, close after.

> ⚠️ No interior shutoff? Install one (cheap), or wrap exterior pipe + faucet with foam insulation + faucet cover (~$5). Cover alone is stopgap, not substitute.

### Foam faucet cover (~$5)
Plastic/foam dome sealed to wall. Traps small pocket of house warmth. Useful for non-frost-free without shutoff, or extra insurance in cold climates.

## Part 2 — Exposed interior pipes (insulation)
Any pipe in unheated space (crawlspace, unfinished basement near rim joist, exterior wall, garage).
- **Slit-foam tubes** (~$1/ft) snap over pipe. Cut w/ utility knife; tape slits.
- Cover full exposed run incl. elbows + valves (miter-cut at corners).
- Pipes near exterior walls + rim joists = coldest spots — prioritize.
- Genuinely cold space (unheated crawlspace/garage): add [[GLOSSARY#Heat cable (heat tape)|heat cable]] inside foam sleeve. Plug before first freeze. Lifespan ~3–5 yr — inspect/replace when insulation cracks.

### Deep-freeze habit
When temps drop well below normal for your area:
- **Open under-sink cabinets** → warm room air reaches pipes against exterior walls.
- **Drip a cold faucet** served by that pipe (pencil-thin stream) → moving water freezes slower; open faucet relieves pressure if ice forms.
- **Keep garage door closed** if plumbing runs through garage.

## Part 3 — Sprinkler blowout
Underground irrigation holds water → freezes → cracks pipes/valves/heads. [[GLOSSARY#Sprinkler blowout|Blowout]] clears with compressed air before winter.

| | DIY | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Rental ~$50–75/day | ~$65–100/yr |
| Equipment | **High-CFM compressor + regulator** (most homeowner units lack volume) | Commercial-grade |
| Pressure | 50 PSI max PVC / 30 PSI poly | Pro knows spec |
| Risk | High if undersized | Low |

> ⚠️ **DIY catch:** it's CFM (volume), not PSI — small compressor hits 50 PSI but can't move enough air to clear a zone before running out. Residual water freezes → cracked manifold (hundreds to dig/fix). ~$90 pro is cheap insurance.
> **Always open a zone valve before applying air** — pressurizing sealed system is explosive.

## Part 4 — Ice dams (the roof failure)
[[GLOSSARY#Ice dam|Ice dam]] = ridge of ice at roof edge backing meltwater under shingles → leak. Damage catastrophic: saturated insulation, stained ceilings, rotted rafters, mold.

### The cause chain
1. **Heat leaks** into attic from house
2. Warm roof deck melts snow above
3. Meltwater runs down to cold eave
4. Refreezes at eave = ice dam
5. Backed-up water under shingles → leak

> **Weather didn't cause the dam — your house's heat loss did.** Cold roof doesn't melt snow unevenly → no dam. Fix = make roof cold.

### Prevention (in this order)
1. **[[GLOSSARY#Air sealing (attic)|Air sealing]]** — first + most effective. Warm air leaks through gaps around wiring, plumbing, chimneys, light fixtures, attic hatch. Seal with caulk, spray foam, fire-rated foam at chimneys. **Insulation alone doesn't stop air leaks** — fiberglass/cellulose filter air, doesn't block it.
2. **Insulation** — after sealing, bring attic floor to code (R-49 cold climates). Slows conductive heat loss.
3. **Ventilation** — soffit (intake) + ridge/roof (exhaust) vents flush any heat that gets in. **Mechanical attic vent NOT recommended** (UMN Extension). Natural passive, balanced intake-to-exhaust.

> ⚠️ **Air seal FIRST, not insulation first.** Piling insulation without sealing leaks does almost nothing — warm air bypasses through gaps. Air sealing is unglamorous (crawling w/ caulk gun) but the most effective fix. Pro energy audit + blower door finds worst leaks.

### Reactive (stopgap, not fix)
- **[[GLOSSARY#Roof melt sock (ice melt sock)|Roof melt Socks]]** — calcium chloride in porous tube, tossed on roof perpendicular to dam → melts channel. Stopgap.
- **Roof heat cable** — zigzag at eave, creates melt channels. For chronic dams where air-seal isn't feasible (cathedral ceiling). Stopgap.
- **Never chip ice off roof** — damages shingles, fall risk.

## Part 5 — Heating system winter prep
- Replace furnace filter before heating season ([[HVAC Filter Change|filter change]]); check monthly during heavy use.
- **Keep furnace intake/exhaust vents clear of snow** — blocked exhaust pushes CO into house. Check after every heavy snow.
- Book fall pro tune-up before season — gas furnace combustion + heat exchanger inspection matters most.

## Part 6 — Non-water winter tasks
- Reverse ceiling fans clockwise, low speed (push warm air down)
- Weatherstrip/caulk windows + doors (feel for drafts on windy cold day)
- Service/clean fireplace or woodstove before first use (chimney fire risk)
- Bring in hoses, furniture cushions, planters, freeze-sensitive items
- Check generator fuel + run it (winter outages = when you need it)
